How to spot fake reviews of nail salons in Da Nang

Almost every salon in the tourist quarters shows 4.8 to 5.0 stars. The stars cannot tell you much; the reviews behind them can, if you read them in the right order.

How can you tell if a Da Nang nail salon's Google reviews are fake?

Sort the reviews by Newest and read the past three months before you check the average. The signs to watch for are a burst of reviews over a few days following a quiet spell, several five-star reviews posted minutes apart, a high share of stars without text, reviewers with only one review and no photo, near-identical wording, and offers of a discount in return for a review, which Google's rules do not allow. A single sign proves nothing by itself. Several together deserve a second look.

Read them in date order first

Open the salon on Google Maps, tap Reviews and switch the sort to Newest. Then scroll back three months. In My An, a busy salon gets a review every day or two, spread over the week and written in several languages, with the occasional four-star among the fives. Faking that rhythm for long is hard.

Next, read the lowest ratings. The way the owner replies to a complaint says more about the visit you will have than fifty more five-star reviews.

A burst after a quiet spell

Look out for a sudden run of reviews (say fifteen in two days) after weeks with almost none, or for a steady flow that stops dead. Real customers do not coordinate. When five-star reviews come in clusters posted within minutes of one another, that is one of the clearer signs of a campaign.

Stars with nothing to say

Leaving a star rating without text is allowed, and many genuine customers do it. A page on which a large share of the recent five-star reviews carry no text at all is another matter: such a review costs nothing to post and says nothing you can check.

Reviewers with no history

Tap on a reviewer's name. One review, no photo and a generic name make an account weak evidence, particularly when several such accounts turn up in the same week. The opposite is a traveller who reviews hotels and restaurants during the same trip.

The same sentence twice

Read five recent reviews one after another. They may have been written from a template if they share phrasing, put the compliments in the same order, or praise the same technician in the same words.

Rewards for reviews

Certain salons offer a discount, a free add-on or a gift in exchange for a Google review. That is not allowed under Google's review policy. A rating built on rewards is inflated even when every reviewer was a real customer, and the same applies to asking only happy customers for a review: an honest salon asks everyone.

A count that suddenly drops

Google takes down reviews that it judges to break its rules. If a salon's review count drops by dozens in a matter of days, many reviews have been removed. Which ones, you will not see. Still, it is a reason to read the recent reviews that remain with care.

What genuine reviews look like

Specifics. The technician's name, the treatment, what it cost, how long it took, a photo of the nails a few days afterwards, a complaint about the wait with an answer from the owner. Genuine review pages are slightly uneven, which is reassuring.

How this guide uses review data

Each ranking on this site prints every salon's Google rating and review count alongside the date of the snapshot they are taken from, while our comparison pages show how a salon's review count changed from one snapshot to the next. The methodology page sets out how a rating is weighed against the number of reviews behind it. For anything no review can tell you, use the 90-second check at the door.

The short version

A long, steady history of specific reviews deserves more trust than a perfect score. Sort by Newest, read the low ratings and walk away from any salon that offers you something in return for a review. Every salon's figures, with their date, appear in our ranking of Da Nang nail salons.

Frequently asked questions

Are Google reviews of Da Nang nail salons reliable?

Mostly, for salons with a long and steady review history. Read the newest reviews and the low ratings before trusting a 5.0, and be wary of sudden bursts.

Is a salon allowed to give a discount for a Google review?

Google's review policy does not allow offering anything in exchange for a review, including discounts or free add-ons. A salon can ask every customer for an honest review, with no reward and without asking only the happy ones.

What should I do if I think a review is fake?

Use Report review on Google Maps for that review. Then judge the salon in person with the 90-second check: single-use tools, a working steriliser, prices in writing.
