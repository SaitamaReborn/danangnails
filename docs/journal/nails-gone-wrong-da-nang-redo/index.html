Làm lại: what happens when you tell a Da Nang salon the nails are wrong

One of the 151 salons in this guide rates below 4.5 and 59 show a flat five, so the star column warns you about nothing. The 22 reviews at three stars or less are the whole public record of failure, and sorted by when the customer spoke, they say the same thing: the remedy tracks the timing.

In short
  • The rating column cannot warn you, and the arithmetic explains why. Across the 151 nail salons in this guide and 53,433 Google ratings, the average is 4.89. Of those salons, 118 sit at 4.9 or better and 59 show a flat 5.0, while exactly one is below 4.5: ZozoNails Studio at 106 Lê Lợi, 4.4 across 453 ratings. Suzy Nails on Trần Phú holds 10,343 ratings; if its average is exactly 4.9, it would take about 130 one-star reviews in a row before the listing reads 4.8.
  • Twenty-two of the 715 review texts behind this guide sit at three stars or below, twelve of those at one star. Sort the 22 by where the customer raised the problem, and the remedy follows from that. Mention it in the chair and a nail gets refiled or repainted. At the counter, somebody may waive a service or knock 100K off. Say it weeks later in the review box, though, and you get a reply from the owner and nothing more.
  • The redo window that exists is sized for a resident. A customer at Liti Nail & Hair 2, 08 An Thượng 39 reports five days to come back if you are not satisfied, and Trà Beauty on Trần Cao Vân answered a seven-day chip with a warranty plus 10% off the next visit. Tuoi Tre News reported on 18 October 2026, quoting standing vice-chairman Ho Ky Minh, that international visitors averaged under two days per trip over the first nine months of 2026. Your appointment is the warranty.
  • This piece rests on one opinion: inspect before the top coat, and tip after you've looked. Consider the traveller who tipped a salon that had botched her hands and feet and then watched the papered-over spots surface by evening, and the customer who tipped on day one at Suzy Nails and got a 25-minute pedicure at the cat-eye price on day two. Both paid extra to be polite first.

Why the star column cannot warn you

Sorting this city by rating runs into a problem. Between them, the 151 nail salons in this guide carry 53,433 Google ratings, with an average of 4.89. One hundred and eighteen show 4.9 or higher. Fifty-nine show a flat 5.0. Only one salon, ZozoNails Studio at 106 Lê Lợi in Hải Châu, sits under 4.5, at 4.4 across 453 ratings. Rank the city by stars and you get a 59-way tie at the top of the list.

Big denominators are the mechanism. Suzy Nails & Foot Massage at 109 Trần Phú is the most-reviewed nail salon in this guide with 10,343 ratings at 4.9. Assume that average is exactly 4.9, and roughly 130 consecutive one-star reviews would be needed to pull the displayed figure down to 4.8. A furious customer cannot dent it, so the number on the listing is a measure of how long a shop has been busy rather than of what it did last Tuesday. Mei Nail at 5 Nguyễn Duy Hiệu shows 4.9 across 105 ratings, with a review in it from a customer who says her nails were ground thinner than paper. Nhung Brows & Nails on Nguyễn Phước Nguyên shows 4.8 across 22.

The text is also a sample rather than a census. Google's Places API hands this guide five reviews per salon, so the 715 texts behind these pages are about 1.3% of the ratings count, and they are the five Google chooses to surface. That 687 of the 715 are five-star says something about Google's selection as well as about the work. The dates on this data exist for that reason, and the snapshot behind this piece is 2 October 2026.

The only warning anywhere in the dataset, then, is prose, and all of it sits in 22 reviews. As verdicts they are not much use, since most name a shop that 300 other people loved. Read for sequence instead and they become the best thing in the corpus, because each records what the customer said, when she said it and what the salon did next. That sequence can be learned. It is worth more than any star. Review text is already the only signal worth reading in this city; this is the part of it nobody mines.

Twenty-two reviews, sorted by when somebody spoke

The 22 break down as twelve one-star, five two-star and five three-star. The failures inside them are repetitive: a paid service shortened or skipped, a file job that came out uneven, polish that lifted within days, blood, and a bill nobody agreed in advance. What differs between them is not the fault. It is the moment the customer opened her mouth, and there are only three of those: in the chair with the work in progress, at the counter with cash in hand, and in the review box after the flight home.

Grouped that way the dataset is almost tidy. Problems raised while the technician was still working got fixed, refiled or repainted in most cases, free. Problems raised at payment produced a waived service once, a 100,000đ reduction once, and a flat refusal twice. Problems raised in the review box produced replies from owners and no remedy at all. Exactly one review of the 715 describes cash going back across a counter, and the customer who got it had smudged her own polish.

The clearest illustration is a five-star review for a salon that did nothing wrong. A traveller from the United States wrote up Rabbit & Co. Nails, 45 Trương Quyền in Sơn Trà, 47 ratings, about three weeks before this guide's October snapshot. Her account: the salon she had used the previous day botched both her hands and her toes, she spoke up more than once while the work was still going on, and instead of correcting anything the technicians covered the faults and carried on. She tipped on the way out because she likes supporting small businesses. By evening every patched spot had surfaced, and the next day she paid a second salon to take the lot off, extensions, acrylic, cat eye and toes. She never names the first shop, so neither can this guide.

At the opposite end sits the complaint that comes too late to be anything more than a record. A customer at CIAO NAIL STUDIO, 22 Mai Thúc Lân, 652 ratings, says the pedicure package her friend had booked included cuticle care, and that this step was skipped entirely, so all that was left was a gel removal and a new colour. They raised it politely, through Google Translate into Vietnamese, and got an eye-roll. The owner replied to the review, and the customer added a rebuttal to her own review in answer. Two careful statements, published and permanent, and no cuticle work for anybody. The cuticle half of the appointment is worth knowing in advance, since it is the line most often dropped quietly when a shop runs late.

In the chair: the refile nobody asks for

This window is a physical one. Under the lamp, gel cures one layer at a time. So changing the shape costs nothing before the base coat goes on, changing colour costs a wipe and two minutes before the top coat, and afterwards any change at all costs a full removal. Removal runs 10K to 150K across the six published menus on the price table, which puts the gap between speaking up at minute fifteen and at minute ninety at roughly the price of the whole appointment again.

Trà Beauty at 777 Trần Cao Vân, 27 ratings, is the case where asking got nowhere, and it is worth reading because the customer is a local who has a set done every three to four weeks. By her account she pointed out during filing that her middle finger had come out shorter than the rest and visibly off, was told that the technician could see nothing uneven, and when the owner was called in, got the same answer again. She had the work removed and left after two and a half hours. On an earlier visit she had flagged air bubbles under the first box nail as it was fitted, and says four or five of the ten finished the same way.

Shops that run a check-in habit show up repeatedly in the five-star text, and they are not the expensive ones. At Liti Nail & Hair 2 on An Thượng 39, a customer paying 1.2 million for a gel-x set and a pedicure notes that the two technicians redid a couple of designs she herself thought were fine, and that they gave her the total before starting. At Minhy Nails 2, 46 Phạm Tu, 98 ratings, a self-described difficult customer writes that the staff watch for the moment you are unhappy and fix it then, across a real language gap. At Oanh Beauty & Nail, 210 Trần Duy Chiến, the technician checked in during the manicure, spoke no English, worked through Google Translate and took an hour and a quarter over a removal and a copied design. At Saigon Nail Art on Nguyễn Xuân Khoát, a reviewer credits Annie with confirming at every stage.

Which leads to the blunt version. The quiet customer gets the worse set, every time, because a technician reads silence as approval and because the alternative to asking at minute fifteen is paying for a removal at minute ninety. A reference photo on your phone makes this easier, not harder: it converts a feeling into a comparison, and the photo is already doing more work than your English is.

At the counter: a waived pedicure, 100K, or you can just pay it

One remedy in this dataset was clear-cut, and it came at the till. Eight years old, the ZozoNails review is the oldest complaint in the set. It describes a pedicure where the cuticle was cut until two toes bled, then cleaned with water and cotton until the customer asked whether that was all right, and after that disinfected. On stinging skin, a second technician repainted the same nails three or four times. By her account, once she said how frustrated she was, the manager apologised and waived the cost of the pedicure. Of the 151 salons, that shop is the one now sitting below 4.5.

Time disputes get money, because time cannot be given back. At Remie Nail Spa, 85 Nguyễn Xuân Khoát, a customer with twenty minutes left of a 90-minute massage was wrapped up early when other customers arrived, and the manager took 100,000đ off. At Jisu nail, 115 Trần Phú, 117 ratings, a 45-minute foot massage and scrub ran about 30 minutes because the masseuse left to deal with other customers. Neither customer got the service completed; both were handed a number instead.

And the refusals, which are instructive about how the argument actually goes. At Suzy Nails, 10,343 ratings, a customer paid 300,000đ for a cat-eye manicure that took 50 minutes, tipped, came back the next day for a 300,000đ pedicure, was told her toenails were too small for cat eye, and got plain colour in 25 minutes at the same price. She raised it in the shop and was told that pedicures take less time. No refund was mentioned. At CASA NAIL SPA, 42 Mai Thúc Lân, 326 ratings, a 250K foot-care package sold as five items included a 45-minute massage that by her account ran about eight minutes; she disputes close to 500,000đ of the bill and reports being told that it was not expensive for her, so she could pay. She is explicit that the price menu itself was clear. Getting the total in writing before you sit down is what removes this conversation entirely.

There is one lever in the room and it is the cash in your hand, because you pay at the end and almost always in cash. Use it for work rather than for money. Ask for the skipped step to be done now, the nail refiled now, the missing twenty minutes delivered now. A salon with four technicians can produce labour on the spot; a refund needs an owner, a decision and a loss.

The single refund in these 715 reviews shows the escalation that works, and also how little it is worth counting on. At Lena Nails and Beauty, 211/88 Nguyễn Văn Thoại, 543 ratings, a customer having regular polish instead of gel left before it had dried in the humidity, and smudged it twice, the second time while putting on flip-flops. Tam, as the review names the technician, repainted the nails more than once, then called the owner, and the shop refunded her. This account comes from the customer in the next chair, who wrote it up in defence of the staff. Asking for the owner is the escalation that moves money. Being in the right is, evidently, optional.

Bảo hành is sized for somebody who lives here

Vietnamese salons do warranty gel work, and the free-fix window is usually three to seven days. The two concrete offers in this corpus match that: five days to return at Liti Nail & Hair 2, and at Trà Beauty a warranty plus 10% off the next visit after plain gel chipped at seven days. For a customer getting ten to fifteen sets a year within a motorbike ride, both are real money.

Then there is who actually reads this page. Tuoi Tre News reported on 18 October 2026 that Da Nang drew nearly 7.7 million international overnight visitors in the first nine months of 2026, up more than 28% year on year, and quoted Ho Ky Minh, standing vice-chairman of the city administration, saying the focus is shifting from arrival numbers to experience, longer stays and higher spending, because the average international trip currently runs under two days. A five-day redo window, offered to somebody who lands Friday and flies Sunday, is decoration.

What the warranty looks like once you have left the chair is on the listing of NONI NAILS, 17 Phạm Phú Thứ, 368 ratings at 4.7. A customer's yellow gel started peeling the day after it was painted. She raised it repeatedly, was told the problem was that she had not kept her hand still, and by her account staff discussed her in Vietnamese in front of her. The shop agreed to redo one nail, the right thumb, which came out fine with a different shade, and charged the full price. The rest of the yellow chipped off.

That is why, for a visitor, the realistic fix is the second salon and not the first. Here the repair trade is visible and cheap. Reviews credit Missha 2 at 48 An Thượng 3 with correcting a badly done set, She Nails at 113 Trần Duy Chiến with redoing another shop's work, SORA K-NAIL STUDIO at 76 Phan Châu Trinh with repairing a broken nail that another place had already failed to fix, and Chik Nails at 19 Nguyễn Duy Hiệu with a removal, a new set, a pedicure and a redesign for about 30 Canadian dollars. Set against city ranges of 10K to 150K for removal and 70K to 300K for gel colour, starting over costs less than a decent dinner on An Thượng, and much less than another hour spent arguing with people who have already told you your hands are the problem.

Three checkpoints, and four words

Checkpoint one, after filing and before any product. Put both hands flat on the table, palms down, and look along the row from the wrist end. Lengths that look even from above are often not, and the single most common shaping complaint in this dataset is one finger shorter or sitting crooked. The words are short: lệch for crooked, chưa đều for not even yet, ngắn quá for too short. This is the free checkpoint, and it is the one the Trà Beauty customer tried to use.

Checkpoint two: once the first hand is coloured, before the second one is started. Check the cuticle line for shine sitting on skin rather than nail. Gel on skin lifts as the skin moves, and it takes the edge of the set along with it. Have làm lại giúp mình nhé ready, the sentence that asks for it to be done again. Locals choose the pronoun by relative age, em or chị; mình sidesteps the choice and nobody minds. Say it before the lamp goes on, not after.

Checkpoint three is before the top coat. Chrome, the direction of the cat eye, the drawn line on the accent nail: everything is adjustable at this point, and in ten minutes it becomes a removal job. Pain sits in the same bracket. It is not a test of tolerance. Three of the 22 reviews involve blood or a nail filed down to nothing: at ZozoNails on Lê Lợi; at Nhung Brows & Nails on Nguyễn Phước Nguyên, where the customer says the owner and an apprentice worked on her together; and at Mei Nail on Nguyễn Duy Hiệu, where a customer who had booked a specific service the day before says she was told on arrival that no technician could do it, and her old set got ground down anyway. Đau means it hurts. That one syllable stops any drill in this city.

Then pay, then look again, then tip. In that order, because both tips in this dataset that went out over work nobody had fixed were followed by something worse: faults surfacing all evening in one case, a 25-minute pedicure at full price the next day in the other. And if the thing does go wrong and you write it up, copy the two longest complaints here rather than the angriest: both come from locals who get a set every three or four weeks, and both name the step that failed, the minute it failed and what was said next. That is the review that helps the next reader. The door checks are a separate job, and they catch a different set of problems.

Frequently asked

What should I do if I don't like my nails at a Da Nang salon?

Say so while the technician is still working, because the evidence says that is the only point at which anything gets fixed. Across the 715 public reviews behind this guide, 22 sit at three stars or lower, and in them problems raised in the chair were refiled or repainted free, problems raised at the counter produced a waived service once and a 100,000đ reduction once, and problems raised later in the review box produced owner replies and no remedy. Nobody in this dataset reports money returned after leaving the shop. The practical windows are after filing and before product, after the first hand is coloured, and before the top coat cures: at that last point a fix still costs two minutes, and afterwards it costs a full removal, which runs 10K to 150K on this guide's price table. A traveller who reviewed Rabbit & Co. Nails in Sơn Trà describes speaking up more than once at the salon she used the day before, being papered over rather than corrected, and paying a second salon to strip everything the following day.

Will a Da Nang nail salon redo nails for free?

Usually yes, and some do it unprompted. Vietnamese salons warranty gel work, typically for three to seven days, and the specific offers visible in this guide's review corpus are five days to come back at Liti Nail and Hair 2, 08 An Thượng 39, and a warranty plus 10% off the next visit at Trà Beauty on Trần Cao Vân after plain gel chipped at seven days. One customer at Liti Nail and Hair 2 reports technicians redoing a couple of designs she thought were already fine, and at Minhy Nails 2 on Phạm Tu a reviewer says staff watch for the moment you look unhappy and fix it then. The limit is the calendar. Tuoi Tre News reported on 18 October 2026 that international visitors to Da Nang average under two days per trip, so for most visitors a five-day window expires after the flight. Treat the appointment itself as the warranty and inspect before the top coat.

Can you get a refund at a Da Nang nail salon?

Rarely. Of the 715 reviews behind this guide, exactly one describes a refund, and a bystander wrote it. At Lena Nails and Beauty, 211/88 Nguyễn Văn Thoại, a customer who left before her regular polish was dry smudged it twice; the technician repainted it more than once, then called the owner, and the shop gave her the money back. More common is a charge waived on the spot. A customer at ZozoNails Studio on Lê Lợi whose cuticles were cut until two toes bled says that once she said how frustrated she was, the manager apologised and waived the pedicure. At Remie Nail Spa on Nguyễn Xuân Khoát, a 90-minute massage finished twenty minutes early and the manager took 100,000đ off. On the other side, a customer at Suzy Nails on Trần Phú paid 300,000đ for a 25-minute pedicure done in plain colour instead of the cat eye she had been given the day before, was told that pedicures take less time, and was offered no refund; a customer at CASA NAIL SPA on Mai Thúc Lân who disputes close to 500,000đ reports hearing that the amount was not expensive for her. Ask for the work instead, meaning the skipped step done now and the nail refiled now. What these shops can produce immediately is labour.

How do I tell a Vietnamese nail technician to redo a nail?

Làm lại giúp mình nhé means please do it again for me. In Vietnamese the pronoun depends on relative age, em for someone younger and chị for an older woman, so a local would say giúp em or giúp chị; mình avoids the choice, and nobody will mind. Four more words cover most of what goes wrong. Lệch is crooked or off-centre, chưa đều is not even, dày quá is too thick, and đau means it hurts, which ought to stop a drill or a cuticle nipper at once. Among the 22 low-rated reviews in this guide's corpus, three involve bleeding or a nail filed too thin, so that last word counts for more than politeness. When the words fail, point: at the nail, then at the reference photo on your phone, then back at the nail. Reviewers at Oanh Beauty and Nail on Trần Duy Chiến and at CIAO NAIL STUDIO on Mai Thúc Lân both describe putting the whole conversation through Google Translate, with opposite results.
