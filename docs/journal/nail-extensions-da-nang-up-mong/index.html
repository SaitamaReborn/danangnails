Úp móng or đắp bột: why "extensions" runs 60K at one Da Nang salon and 550K at another

Twenty-seven of the 715 reviews behind this guide ask for extensions. Six of them say which kind. On the public menus the city price table rests on, that one word spans 60,000 to 550,000 đồng · a nine-fold gap hiding two completely different products.

In short
  • Five of the six Da Nang menus behind the price table sell nail extensions, full set, and the line runs 60K to 550K. Nail Bơ's 60K is úp móng, a set of pre-shaped soft-gel tips · at that shop the extensions cost less than its own 70K gel colour. Reborn publishes 280K to 550K, Liti 500K, LENA 400K to 500K, Sunam 400K. Amor Spa does not publish the line at all. Same three words, nine times the money.
  • 38 of the 715 reviews here describe added length, across 36 of the 151 salons · and they use two vocabularies that barely meet. 27 say extension. 17 name acrylic or GelX. Only six do both. A reviewer at YaMe Nail on Đống Đa put the phrase in quotation marks · 400K, an hour and a half · which is the most honest punctuation in the dataset.
  • The words a Da Nang menu uses: úp móng is a pre-shaped tip bonded onto your nail, đắp bột is acrylic powder built up on it. Vietnamese salon-trade guides · nqmedical.vn and Seoul Academy's beauty-school site among them · put úp móng near 100K to 150K a set nationally and đắp bột at 250K to 400K. Not one of the 715 English-language reviews here uses either term.

Twenty-seven reviews ask for extensions. Six say which kind.

Search the 715 public reviews behind this guide for anyone who had length added and 38 come back, across 36 of the 151 salons. Split them by the word they use and something odd appears. Twenty-seven say extension or fake nails and never say what the nails were made of. Seventeen name a product · acrylic, or GelX. Only six reviews do both. Eleven describe the exact material and never once reach for the word extension.

Those are two different customers talking. The person who writes acrylic fill or gel x set has a nail routine at home and is shopping for a technician. The person who writes I got extensions is shopping for length and has left the method entirely to the shop. Da Nang gets a great many of the second kind, because the price makes length an impulse rather than a commitment.

The clearest tell in the whole set is punctuation. A reviewer at YaMe Nail, on Đống Đa in Hải Châu, wrote that she had "nail extension" done for 400,000 đồng in an hour and a half, quotation marks included, as though she were repeating a phrase rather than using one. At Shin Nail on Hồ Biểu Chánh out in Hòa Cường, a visitor's entire description of the service is I do nails extension service. Both were delighted. Neither could tell you what is on their hands.

That is usually fine and occasionally expensive. It is fine because the median Da Nang technician is good and will pick something sensible. It is expensive because extensions is not a product in this city, it is a price bracket, and the bracket is nine times wide.

Úp móng, đắp bột, and the 60K floor

Vietnamese salon menus are more precise than the English word is. Úp móng · literally to lay a nail face-down · means a pre-shaped tip, full-cover, bonded onto the natural nail and cured or glued. Your own nail touches adhesive and nothing else, and the set comes off without much drama. Đắp bột means powder: acrylic mixed and built on the nail by hand, shaped as it goes, which takes real skill and real time. Trade guides aimed at Vietnamese salon owners · nqmedical.vn and the Seoul Academy beauty-school site among them · price úp móng nationally at roughly 100K to 150K a set and đắp bột at 250K to 400K. There is also đắp gel in between, builder gel instead of powder.

Now look at what Da Nang publishes. Five of the six salons behind the price table list a full set of extensions, and the range across them is 60K to 550K. The floor is Nail Bơ, whose úp móng service is a set of soft-gel tips, at 60,000 đồng · which is less than the 70,000 that same shop charges for a plain gel colour. Read that twice. At one Da Nang salon, adding ten nails of length is cheaper than painting the ten you have.

The ceiling is the other product. Reborn Nails & Retreat publishes 280K to 550K, a range rather than a figure because a sculpted set is priced by what it takes. Liti Nail & Hair lists 500K, LENA Nails & Beauty 400K to 500K, Sunam Nail & Spa 400K. Amor Spa publishes gel, pedicure and removal and no extensions line at all, which is its own kind of answer.

So the 60K and the 550K are not the same service at a discount and a premium. One is a tip laid on; the other is a nail built. The soft-gel tip set is the one that has taken over the holiday market here, for the plain reason that it takes an hour and comes off cleanly. The sculpted set is for people who want a shape their own nails will not give them.

Asked for acrylic, got glue

There is exactly one failure mode that belongs to a word this loose, and the dataset contains a clean example of it. At Chuty Nailroom on Trần Phú in Hải Châu, a customer asked for extensions on two broken nails. She was told the shop would use an acrylic method. What she got, she writes, was press-ons and nail glue · not GelX, not acrylic · which is a method that will not last as long. She gave three stars, noted that the whole visit cost her 190,000 đồng, that they threw in two layers of reinforcement gel for nothing, cuticle oil and a hand massage, and that a job quoted at thirty minutes took two hours. She recommends the salon anyway, for the price and the care.

That review is worth more than a complaint, because almost everything in it is good. The shop was gentle, generous and cheap. It simply described one product and supplied another, and the customer only knew because she already knew the difference.

None of which makes press-ons the villain. They are a legitimate product, sold openly here, and Vietnam is one of the places the world's press-ons are actually manufactured · Marie Claire spent part of this year arguing they have become the accessory of 2026. At Hanna Beauty Studio on Trần Quốc Toản, a reviewer who went for a GelX set mentions in passing that the same artist sells custom stick-ons for about four dollars, which is a sane thing to buy and a genuinely good deal. A reusable set you take home and apply yourself for one evening is not a lesser manicure. It is a different purchase. The problem is only ever a tip glued on and billed as a nail built.

The ambiguity survives the set, too. At Chic nails on Nguyễn Xí in Thanh Khê, a customer came back for a refill and had one tip taken off; she has thin nails, the removal left a few scratches, and her explanation is the telling part · it was hard to tell what was the tip and what was only soft gel. In the same review she doubts the BIAB she booked was really BIAB rather than some hard-and-soft-gel combination. She is grateful and gives five stars. She still does not know what was on her hands, at either end of the three weeks.

An hour and ten, or five hours

If nobody will tell you which product you are getting, the clock will. A pre-shaped tip set is about an hour. A sculpted set is a half-day. The reviews here bear that out with unusual precision.

At the fast end, a visitor to Hanii Nail on An Cư 1, a street back from My Khe, had a full set of extensions with nail art done in about an hour and ten minutes for 550,000 đồng, off a reference photo, from a technician she says speaks very little English. The YaMe Nail customer on Đống Đa: 400,000, an hour and a half, booked over Instagram, and she volunteers the word speedy.

At the slow end, Hehe Nail Room on Phan Châu Trinh quoted about 750,000 đồng for taking old extensions off and building a new set. The reviewer arrived early, at half past twelve, and left at half past five · five hours. She gave four stars, said the nails matched her reference photo exactly, and the single thing she wanted other people to know is that she had budgeted four hours at most. At Lalla Nail on An Thượng 29, a party of three · acrylic with a cartoon design and a pedicure for one, builder gel for her daughter, heel care for her husband · ran about three hours with two technicians working on her at once.

Which means the quote and the duration have to agree. If a shop offers you a sculpted set in an hour, or an hour's work for 550K, one of those two numbers is describing a different service. Two technicians on one customer is the usual lever for shortening any of this, and it is normal here rather than a favour · but it shortens a three-hour job to ninety minutes, it does not turn đắp bột into úp móng.

The extension line is not the bill

Here is the arithmetic nobody does in advance. A customer at DzonDzon Nails, on 2 Tháng 9 in Hải Châu, wrote her bill out in full: 50K for the manicure, 300K for the gel extensions, 200K for the gel colour, 200K for the nail art, plus 50K for a pedicure and another 200K for gel on the toes. That is one million đồng, and the extensions · the thing she thought she was buying · were 300K of it. Thirty per cent. She gave two stars, said the technician was attentive and detailed and that she was really happy with the quality of the work, and explained that she would probably have chosen differently had she known the total. Nothing in that review says she was overcharged. It says nobody told her the number.

The price table shows why the extension line is such a poor guide to the bill. Nail art runs 5K to 120K per nail across the salons that publish it. Ten fingers of the expensive kind is 1,200K on its own, which outruns the most expensive full set in the city. Gel colour is a separate 70K to 300K. Removal, if you arrived wearing something, is another 10K to 150K. The set is one line among four or five, and it is rarely the biggest.

The reviews at the same altitude make the point by contrast. At CIAO NAIL STUDIO 2 on Nguyễn Thông, a customer paid 850,000 đồng for extensions with gel polish, a complex design and charms, posted the inspiration photo beside the result, and gave five stars. At Liti Nail & Hair 2 on An Thượng 39, a GelX set and a pedicure came to 1.2 million, and the review is glowing · partly because the techs went past closing time and redid designs she thought were already fine, but she names two other things first. They told her the total before starting the service. And the shop gives you five days to come back if you are not happy.

One million for two stars, 1.2 million for five. The work was good in both cases. The difference is entirely whether anyone said the figure out loud before the lamp went on. So ask for the total and not the rate, ask with the art counted per nail, and ask in the message where you book rather than in the chair.

Thin is the tell · and what I would not buy three days before a flight

Method matters less than application, and the dataset is blunt about it. At CHUBBY NAILS on Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai, a customer had acrylic extensions, spent the following week packing and carrying boxes, knocked her hand into one, and came back to report three weeks with no lifting at all. At Lee Nails & Spa on Tô Hiến Thành, another had acrylic too; her index finger broke after a week while she was scratching her arm, there was acrylic left under the free edge bothering her, and dust had cured in under the nail where the lamp set it. Same product. Opposite outcomes. The variable is the hand holding the brush.

What good work looks like, in the words of people who got it: at ZozoNails on Lê Lợi, a reviewer says the artist was careful putting the acrylic on and the nail came out so thin she was shocked · meant as praise, and it is the right praise. At CHIARRA NAIL STUDIO on An Thượng 29, a customer who gets acrylics regularly had a thick application from a salon in Bali that her Da Nang technician had to file back into shape. Thin and even is the whole craft. Thick is what you get when someone is covering for a bad fit at the cuticle.

The other thing to watch is heat. At Chic nails on Nguyễn Xí, a reviewer singles out a technician who did not overfile and who checked whether her nail bed was overheating under the lamp. That is a two-second question and almost nobody in 715 reviews mentions being asked it, which tells you how good a sign it is when you are.

And the flag, which is the reason this piece exists. If you are flying home within three days, buy the tip set and not the sculpted one. The soft-gel tips at the bottom of that 60K to 550K range soak off in about fifteen minutes, which means you can have them taken off here for 10K to 150K on your last morning and get on the plane with your own nails. Acrylic and builder have to be filed down, which is a job you will either pay a stranger at home three times the price to do or · and this is the actual outcome · pick at for a fortnight. A 550K sculpted set in a city where a salon that answers questions about its tools in a sentence is easy to find is a fine purchase on day two of three weeks. It is a bad one on the way to the airport.

Frequently asked

How much do nail extensions cost in Da Nang?

60K to 550K for a full set, across the five Da Nang salons behind this guide's price table that publish the line. The spread is that wide because the words cover two products. The floor is Nail Bơ's úp móng, a set of pre-shaped soft-gel tips, at 60,000 đồng · less than the 70,000 the same shop charges for a plain gel colour. The ceiling is sculpted work: Reborn Nails & Retreat publishes 280K to 550K, Liti Nail & Hair 500K, LENA Nails & Beauty 400K to 500K, Sunam Nail & Spa 400K. Reviewers land inside that range · 300K at a My Khe salon for extensions with a polka-dot design, 400K at YaMe Nail, 550K at Hanii Nail with art included. Remember that the set is one line of four or five: gel colour is another 70K to 300K, nail art is 5K to 120K per nail, and removal is 10K to 150K.

What is the difference between úp móng and đắp bột?

Úp móng means a pre-shaped full-cover tip bonded onto your natural nail · GelX and soft-gel tips are the usual version. Your own nail touches only adhesive, the set takes about an hour, and it soaks off in roughly fifteen minutes. Đắp bột means acrylic powder mixed and built up on the nail by hand, shaped as the technician works, which needs more skill and considerably more time, and has to be filed off rather than soaked. Đắp gel is the same idea with builder gel instead of powder. Vietnamese salon-trade guides price úp móng nationally around 100K to 150K a set and đắp bột at 250K to 400K. Worth knowing because the English word extensions covers all three, and not one of the 715 English-language reviews behind this guide uses either Vietnamese term.

How long do nail extensions take in Da Nang?

About an hour for a pre-shaped tip set, up to a half-day for sculpted. The reviews are specific: a full set with nail art done in an hour and ten minutes at a salon a street back from My Khe, an hour and a half at YaMe Nail in Hải Châu, around three hours at a My An salon for acrylic plus art plus a pedicure with two technicians working at once, and five hours at a Hải Châu nail room for old extensions off and a new set built · that customer had budgeted four and said so. Use the clock as a cross-check on the quote. If a shop offers a sculpted set in an hour, or wants 550K for an hour's work, one of those numbers is describing a service you did not ask for.

Should I get GelX or acrylic extensions on a Da Nang holiday?

GelX or soft-gel tips, in almost every case, and the deciding factor is the flight home rather than the look. Soft-gel tips soak off in about fifteen minutes, so you can have them removed here for 10K to 150K on your last morning and travel with your own nails intact. Acrylic and builder gel have to be filed down, which means paying far more to have it done properly at home, or picking at it for a fortnight and taking keratin with it. Acrylic is not the weaker product · one reviewer put a set through a week of carrying boxes with no lifting at all, while another broke a nail after seven days · the difference is the application, not the method. Ask for thin and even, and if a technician checks whether your nail bed is overheating under the lamp, you are in good hands.
