Forty-four per cent of the complaints come from below the ankle

Search the 715 public reviews behind this guide for feet, toes or a pedicure and 160 come back, spread across 87 of the 149 salons. That is more than one review in five, and it is the largest single seam in the dataset · bigger than nail art, bigger than removal, bigger than price.

It is also the worst-behaved. The house average here is 674 five stars out of 715, a shade over 94%. The pedicure subset runs 142 out of 160, just under 89%. That gap sounds trivial until you turn it around: the whole guide contains 41 reviews that are not five stars, and 18 of them mention feet. Forty-four per cent of every complaint in this dataset comes out of twenty-two per cent of the reviews.

Then read the 18 and the shape of the thing changes again, because almost none of them is about the toenail. Three are about feet that were never washed. Two are about instruments. One is about a 45-minute service that ran about thirty. One is about gel on the toes that had not cured and peeled on the way to the airport. One is about a man who asked four times for a pedicure in an empty shop and was sent out with a manicure. The complaints on hands in this guide tend to be aesthetic · a shape missed, a colour off. The complaints on feet are procedural, which means they are predictable, which means they are avoidable.

The geography is unremarkable and worth saying anyway. Eighty-three of the 160 come from Hải Châu, where 69 of the 149 salons are, 42 from the My Khe beachfront and 28 from the An Thượng grid. Feet are not a beach-strip specialism. The best heel work in this dataset is being done in the working half of the city.

Nobody is going to soak your feet

Pink Nail Studio at 167 Trần Phú carries 2,020 Google reviews at 4.9, six floors above a shop. One customer writes that she was surprised they do not soak your feet before a pedicure · that they take the old polish off and go straight in, and that a scrub is an add-on with its own charge. Five stars. She loved the nails. Her husband had the scrub with no polish and was impressed.

Now the same fact, one star. At Oasis Spa & Nail on Nguyễn Văn Thoại, 674 reviews, a customer booked an express pedicure, was not soaked, and opens her review by asking whether the ratings are fake. At Jeju Spa at 90/8 Trần Phú, 66 reviews, a customer says the pedicure skill was genuinely there but she was not allowed to wash her feet beforehand · three stars. At CIAO NAIL STUDIO 2 on Nguyễn Thông, 112 reviews, a customer paid 200,000đ, loved the result, and took a star off because for that money she had expected a soak or a light exfoliation.

One procedure, four ratings, from one star to five. The only variable is what each customer thought the word covered.

So here is the thing to understand before you sit down. In Britain or Australia the soak is inside the price because labour is the expensive part and warm water is the cheap part, so it gets bundled in as atmosphere. In Da Nang labour is the affordable part, and everything is therefore itemised. Amou Nail studio at 175 Nguyễn Chí Thanh, 279 reviews, prints the whole logic on one bill: French pedicure 290K a head, foot spa 60K a head, and the customer's review describes the foot bath, the exfoliation and the mask she got for that 60K. Two dollars. At ZozoNails on Lê Lợi a customer bought an herbal footbath and massage combo alongside her gel pedicure as a separate line.

The word is ngâm chân, soaking the feet, and most menus carry it in English as foot spa or foot bath. Nothing is being withheld from you. You have not been sold it.

115K and 380K are the same word

The reported bills in this dataset spread further on pedicures than on any other service. At S.O.Cnail, 55 Nguyễn Hoàng, 538 reviews at a flat five, a customer itemises 389,000đ for her manicure and 115,000đ for her pedicure. At Lena Nails and Beauty on Phạm Tu, 935 reviews, a customer paid 380,000đ for hers · French, blue tip, old polish removed as part of it. Same noun. More than three times the money.

Everything else lands between. BIJOU on Tạ Mỹ Duật: manicure and pedicure, 160K for both. RiKa on An Nhơn 6: 190K for both, in gel. THAO NAILS on Nguyễn Chí Thanh, 1,890 reviews and three seats: Combo 4 is a basic manicure, a pedicure and fifteen minutes of foot massage for 180K a person. Chuty Nailroom on Trần Phú: 350K for the pair. HM Nail on Đặng Thai Mai: a basic gel mani-pedi under 400K. Nails 4U on Đống Đa: a combo promotion at 600K plus 100K to make the tips French, booked by Instagram message, done in an hour and a half.

Line those up against this guide's own tables and the split is obvious. The spa pedicure rituals are sold by duration · 250K for forty minutes, 380K for fifty-five, 450K for sixty-five, 590K for the seventy-five-minute signature with hot stones. Gel colour on toes is about 180K on top of any of them. A standalone thirty-minute foot and calf massage is about 190K on its own.

The crossing point sits around 200K, and it is the most useful number in this article. Below it you are buying a technician's hands on ten toenails for twenty-five minutes, and that is a fair transaction that will leave your feet exactly as dusty as they arrived. Above 250K you are buying minutes and water, and the menu will tell you how many of each. The disappointment in this dataset lives entirely in the space between those two products, bought by people who thought they were the same one.

One caveat on the gel. A reviewer at R Nail & Spa 3 on Võ Nguyên Giáp had a gel pedicure that had not cured properly, peeled, and was still wet underneath · she flew out that day and could not go back. Toe gel is the last thing done, on the least visible part of you, usually while somebody else is being started. If you are buying it, watch the lamp time, and buy it early in the trip rather than late.

Chà gót: the part actually worth the money

Twenty-three of the 160 name callus removal, heel work, dead skin or exfoliation, across 15 salons, and the tone of those 23 is different from anything else in the guide. People do not describe heel work the way they describe a manicure. They describe it the way you describe getting a tooth fixed.

The trade advertises it in its own shop names, which is the clearest signal in the whole dataset that this is a real specialism rather than an add-on. Thuỳ Thỏ Nail at 17 An Thượng 27, 1,718 reviews, trades as Heel Scrub - Foot Massage in its Google title. Azit Nail on Nguyễn Thái Học, 1,327 reviews, carries 각질제거 · Korean for callus removal. 1998.nailsroom at 172/5 Trần Phú carries 발각질 and 풋스파, foot callus and foot spa. R Nail & Spa 3 on Võ Nguyên Giáp puts Heel Scrub in the same breath as nails, massage and lashes. Eleven of the 149 salons name foot work in their trading title; 52 carry a spa, massage or foot word somewhere in it.

What it actually is: an electric sanding bit run over the dry heel and the ball of the foot, sometimes after a soak and sometimes not. A regular at CHUBBY NAILS on Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai, 223 reviews, describes exactly that · an electric sandpaper filer taken over the heels and the dry patches, and says it is the thing that brings her back. A customer at Nail By Nhỏ Studio on Ông Ích Khiêm called the callus treatment a brand-new pair of feet, and took her one star off because the room she was put in had no air conditioning. At Đà Nẵng Forest on Võ Nguyên Giáp, 4,410 reviews, a woman who booked a spa afternoon for herself, her mother, her aunt and her sister says the one thing she would insist on is the scrub. Cong Nail on Trần Phú prices a heel scrub around 150K.

And here is why it belongs in a Da Nang guide specifically rather than a general one. You walk here, in sandals, on hot pavement, in construction dust. A customer at Jisu nail on Trần Phú works it out mid-review: she had been averaging fifteen thousand steps a day exploring the city and realised it was time. A first-timer at Nails 4U on Đống Đa writes that she was embarrassed about how dusty and dead-skinned her feet were, and that the staff kept telling her it was okay.

The opinion, and it is the whole point of the article. If you have one afternoon and 300K, put it into the heel and skip the toe colour. The colour lasts three weeks and photographs at arm's length. The heel work lasts about the same and you feel it every time you put a sandal on, which in this city is every day of the trip.

Lấy khóe, and the drill that goes from feet to hands

There is one step on a Vietnamese pedicure with no equivalent on a Western menu, and it is worth knowing the name of before somebody starts. Lấy khóe is digging out the corner of the toenail where it turns into the skin fold. It is not cuticle work · it goes deeper, into the groove at the side of the nail, and it is done because it stops the nail growing into the flesh.

Vinmec, the Vietnamese hospital group, publishes patient guidance on exactly this. Its account of what goes wrong is short: unsanitary tools, cutting too deep, taking out too much tissue. The consequences it lists run from swelling, redness and pus through bacterial and fungal infection to tissue necrosis in the severe cases, and its own advice is to cut the nail straight and leave one or two millimetres rather than chase the corner. It tells you to see a doctor if there is no improvement after seven days.

The one-star at ZozoNails on Lê Lợi, 453 reviews at 4.4, is that guidance in practice. The customer describes her big toe and a middle toe cut until they bled during cuticle work, cleaned with water and cotton until she asked whether it was alright, and only then disinfected. Elsewhere in the 715 there is a single one-star describing an infection appearing on two toes the day after an appointment. One out of 715 is not an epidemic. It is also not zero, and the mechanism is not mysterious.

The tool is the other half of it. At Cong Nail on Trần Phú, 389 reviews, a two-star review records the specific thing to watch for: the same drill used on her husband's feet went on to another customer's manicure with nothing done to it in between. Same complaint in different words at Jeju Spa · pedicure skill fine, tools not properly sanitised.

The counter-examples are just as specific, and they are what a good room looks like. At Lalla Nail on An Thượng 29, 1,026 reviews at a flat five, a customer names the dry heat steriliser and notes that every instrument came out of a sealed pouch in front of her. The Amou reviewer says the same about freshly sterilised tools. This is the ordinary 90-second check at the door with one addition for feet: watch the bit, not the handpiece. The drill body is never sterile and does not need to be; the sanding bit touching your heel should come out of a packet or a pouch, and it is the single easiest thing in a salon to verify without asking anyone anything.

If you want the corner left alone, the sentence is không lấy khóe. It is the same principle as the skin work on your hands, one groove deeper and a good deal further from a chemist.

Book his as well, and book it in writing

Fourteen of the 160 foot reviews name a husband, a boyfriend, a partner, a son or a men's service, across fourteen different salons. That is roughly one in eleven, in a trade that markets itself almost entirely to women, and the reviews are not what you would expect.

At 오드리네일앤스파 on Mỹ Khê 6, 3,152 reviews, a man writes that he hesitated about the whole idea, went along with his mother, and had nail and foot care including cuticle and callus removal for the first time · his summary is that his hands and feet feel healthier, which is a sentence about maintenance rather than decoration. At Lena Nails on Phạm Tu a couple book a mani-pedi and a men's pedicure together. At Pink Nail on Trần Phú the husband takes the scrub without polish. At The C Nails, 260 Phan Châu Trinh, 186 reviews, a boyfriend has a manicure and pedicure while he waits, which is a better use of two hours than any phone.

And then the failure, which is the reason this section exists. At Jini nail on Trần Phú, 827 reviews, a couple walked into an empty salon wanting manicures and pedicures. His wife was seated and taken through a gel manicure, a pedicure and a foot scrub. He asked for a pedicure repeatedly, was ignored, and was moved along with a manicure only. Three stars, and the review is more baffled than angry.

Nothing in that review suggests a policy. It suggests a shop that had staffed for one customer and heard one booking. Which is the fix: put it in the message in numbers before you arrive · two people, two pedicures, heel scrub on both · because bookings here happen on WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger and a written line survives the walk to the door in a way a request at reception does not.

There is a case for sending him in first, on his own, the day before you book anything for yourself. A men's pedicure has no colour on it, which strips the service back to the only parts that matter · the soak, the corner, the heel, the tools · and leaves nowhere for a shop to hide. It is the cheapest audit available of whether a salon is any good at feet, and it costs about what a plate of bún chả does.

Frequently asked

Do nail salons in Da Nang soak your feet before a pedicure?

Usually not, unless you buy the soak as a separate item. In Vietnam the word pedicure means the nail work · shaping, the corner of the toenail, cuticles, polish · and the foot bath, scrub and mask are priced individually. A five-star reviewer at a 2,020-review salon on Trần Phú notes that they go straight from removing old polish into the pedicure and that the scrub is an extra charge; a one-star at a beachfront shop on Nguyễn Văn Thoại describes the identical procedure and calls it unhygienic. The same fact, opposite ratings. Ask for it by name: ngâm chân, or foot spa on most English menus. At one Nguyễn Chí Thanh salon that line is 60K · about two dollars · and it buys a foot bath, exfoliation and a mask on top of a 290K French pedicure.

How much does a pedicure cost in Da Nang?

Reported bills in the 715 reviews behind this guide run from 115,000đ to 380,000đ for a service both customers called a pedicure. Combined manicure-and-pedicure prices cluster tighter: 160K for both at a studio on Tạ Mỹ Duật, 180K per person for a mani, pedi and fifteen-minute foot massage combo on Nguyễn Chí Thanh, 190K in gel on An Nhơn 6, 350K on Trần Phú, under 400K on Đặng Thai Mai. The spa pedicure rituals on this guide's price tables are sold by duration instead: about 250K for forty minutes, 380K for fifty-five, 450K for sixty-five and 590K for a seventy-five-minute signature with hot stones, with gel colour for toes around 180K on top. Below roughly 200K you are paying for nail work; above 250K you are paying for minutes and water.

What is a heel scrub at a Da Nang nail salon, and is it worth it?

It is an electric sanding bit taken over the dry heel and the ball of the foot, sometimes after a soak, and it is the local specialism rather than an afterthought. Twenty-three of the 160 foot-related reviews in this guide name callus removal, heel work or dead skin, and four of the 149 salons put heel scrub or the Korean 각질 · callus · directly into their Google business name, including a 1,718-review shop on An Thượng 27 and a 1,327-review shop on Nguyễn Thái Học. Prices reported run around 150K at a Trần Phú salon. It is worth more than the toe colour on a Da Nang trip specifically, because you are walking fifteen thousand steps a day in sandals through construction dust, and you feel the heel every time you put a shoe on.

Can men get a pedicure in Da Nang?

Yes, and fourteen of the 160 foot-related reviews in this guide involve one, across fourteen different salons. A man reviewing a 3,152-review studio on Mỹ Khê 6 describes his first nail and foot care including cuticle and callus removal; a salon on Phạm Tu is described serving a couple a mani-pedi and a men's pedicure together; a customer at a Trần Phú salon reports her husband taking the heel scrub without any polish. Book it in writing rather than adding it at the door, though. The one clear failure in the set is a three-star at an 827-review shop on Trần Phú where a wife was seated straight away and her husband asked repeatedly for a pedicure in an empty salon before being sent out with only a manicure.
