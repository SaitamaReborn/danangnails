Hangul on the sign: what Da Nang's Korean-facing nail shops do differently

26 of the 149 salons in this guide put Korean on the shopfront. Three carry Chinese, two Japanese, one Russian · and not one of those without Hangul beside it. Those 26 hold a median 355 Google reviews against 99 for everyone else.

In short
  • 26 of the 149 salons here carry Korean script in their own trading name. Every other foreign alphabet in the set appears only alongside it · there is no Chinese-only nail sign in Da Nang.
  • They sit inland. Fifteen of the 26 are in Hải Châu, five of those on Trần Phú alone, against five on the My Khe beachfront.
  • Median 355 Google reviews against 99 for the rest of the guide · and a slightly lower average rating, 4.86 against 4.92. That is volume, not perfection.
  • The bundle is different: four of the 26 advertise 이발관, a barbershop, and two advertise 각질 · callus removal · next to the nails.

Twenty-six shopfronts in Hangul. Every other alphabet is a footnote.

Of the 149 businesses in this guide, 26 carry Korean script inside the name Google publishes for them. Now set that against every other foreign alphabet in the set. Chinese characters appear on three shopfronts. Japanese kana on two. Cyrillic on one. And not a single one of those six omits Hangul · the Chinese and the Japanese and the Russian are all riding along on signs that were painted for Korean customers first. There is no Chinese-only nail sign anywhere in this dataset.

The arrivals data explains the first half of that and not the second. South Korea supplied 18.63% of Da Nang's inbound visitors in July 2026, well clear of China at 11.23%, the United States at 6.24%, Australia at 5.86%, Taiwan at 5.69% and Russia at 5.50%. The city counted 12.10 million arrivals across the first seven months of the year, up 24.8% year on year, 6.01 million of them international. So roughly one foreign visitor in five is Korean, and about one nail shopfront in six is signed for them. Two numbers landing that close together reads well and proves nothing by itself.

China is the part worth sitting with. An 11.23% share is a serious block of visitors and it buys no signage at all. Painting a second script on your door is not a play for walk-by trade · that trade walks past anyway. It is a bet on a customer who researches the shop before the flight, messages ahead and comes back next year. Three of these salons publish an open KakaoTalk channel in the slot where a website would go, which is the same bet placed a second time.

The Korean nail trade is inland, on Trần Phú

Split the 26 by area and they land nowhere near where you would guess. Fifteen are in Hải Châu. Five are on the My Khe beachfront, three in My An and An Thượng, two in Sơn Trà, and one sits out past Ngũ Hành Sơn on the Điện Bàn stretch of Võ Nguyên Giáp.

Then narrow it once more. Five of the 26 are on Trần Phú, a riverside street in the old city where this guide lists twelve salons in total. Nearly half the shops on that street are signed in Korean. Nothing about Trần Phú is beachy · it is ten minutes from the sand on a scooter, past the cathedral, in the part of town where people live and work.

Whatever put them there, the consequence for you runs in a useful direction. The price tables put the beachfront band 10–30% above the city rate for identical work, so the most established foreign-facing nail businesses in Da Nang are sitting on the cheaper side of that gradient. Visitors do the opposite by default: they book the salon they can see from the hotel, pay the strip premium, and never find out that the shop with four thousand happy Korean regulars is a 40,000 đồng Grab ride inland.

이발관, 샴푸, 각질 · the menu is not the one you were handed

Read the 26 names as a menu and a different trade appears. Four of them carry 이발관 · barbershop · directly alongside the nails. Ten of the 149 salons in the guide name shampooing or a head wash somewhere in the title. And two of the Korean signs advertise 각질: one as 발각질, foot callus, the other as 각질제거, callus removal. Two further shops put Heel Scrub in their English name.

각질제거 is worth knowing as its own word rather than as a translation. It is dedicated work on the hardened skin of the heel and the ball of the foot, done with a heavy file or a blade, and in the Korean-facing shops it is advertised as a headline service in its own right. On this guide's tables heel treatment is a step inside a pedicure ritual · 250K for the forty-minute express, 590K for the seventy-five-minute signature · and the honest version is that a step inside a ritual gets ritual-sized attention.

That is the thing to actually buy here after a week of sandals and hot pavement, and it is the thing least likely to appear on the English card. Ask for it separately. Point at your heel and say the word · a shop that put 각질 on its own shopfront knows exactly what you are asking for, which is more than can be said for the phrase callus removal delivered at speed.

Eleven thousand reviews, and a slightly worse average

The review counts are the clearest signal in the whole set. Median across the 26 Korean-signed shops: 355 Google reviews. Median across the other 123: 99. Six of the 26 clear a thousand reviews, and only nineteen businesses in the entire guide manage that. The most-reviewed nail salon in Da Nang leads its Google name with 수지네일, trades at 109 Trần Phú in Hải Châu from nine to nine, and carries 11,138 reviews at 4.9 · more than the next two businesses in this guide combined.

The counterpoint is in the ratings, and it is small but real. The 26 average 4.86 against 4.92 for everyone else. Twenty-one of them still sit at 4.9 or better. That gap is what volume does to an average: serve eleven thousand people and a few hundred of them are having a bad day. Here is where I will plant a flag · 4.9 across 11,138 visits is a vastly stronger claim than 5.0 across twelve, and a guide that ranks the second above the first is measuring the wrong thing.

One caution about those names, though. The 26 Korean-signed names run an average of 41.5 characters against 21.7 for the rest of the guide, nearly double. Twenty-two of them embed 네일 · nail · and eight embed 다낭 · Da Nang · as literal keywords, and one Hải Châu listing has bracketed a recommendation tag onto the end of its own business name. That is Google Business optimisation aimed at Korean-language search. It tells you the shop is marketed competently. It tells you nothing whatsoever about the manicure.

What you get if you are not Korean

Start with the limit of the evidence. The 715 reviews collected behind this guide break down as 713 English, two Vietnamese and exactly one Korean · Google's sampling is English-weighted, so the Korean review base that actually built these businesses is invisible in the data I am working from. Treat the numbers above as the English-speaking shadow of something larger.

What does surface in English is consistent enough to plan around. One visitor to a Trần Phú spa describes it as set up primarily for Korean guests and then notes, in the same breath, that the staff handled English without trouble. Another praises how well the staff speak Korean. A third mentions that the room was decorated by the Korean owner. Nobody in the set reports being turned away, and nobody reports a language wall.

So the practical change is small: the default language of the room is not yours, which raises the value of a photograph and lowers the value of a description. Send the reference image ahead of time · messaging beats phoning by a distance here · and bring a second photo of your own hand at the length you actually want.

The design vocabulary travels too, and it is worth arriving with. The beauty press this year has been full of the same Korean techniques: syrup or jelly nails built from sheer layered gel, magnetic line and barcode nails where magnetic gel is pulled into a single stripe of shimmer, and the blurred Korean gradient in cocoa and nude tones. A Korean-facing technician in Hải Châu recognises those on sight. And the pricing is the good news · on this guide's tables cat-eye and chrome are billed as a flat full set at about 180K and ombré or French at about 220K, against plain gel colour at 200K. Magnetic work is therefore one of the cheapest current-looking things you can buy in this city, where hand-painted detail runs 15K to 100K per nail and adds up fast.

The sign tells you about the customer, not about the tools

Be precise about what a Hangul shopfront actually proves. It proves repeat foreign custom. It proves somebody in that room has answered the same four questions several hundred times and has a system for it. It proves the shop is exposed to clients who compare notes with each other in a language you cannot read, which is a real discipline. It proves nothing at all about sterilisation · not one of the 26 signs mentions an autoclave, and the ninety-second check at the door is the same check regardless of which alphabet got you through it.

And the sharper point, which cuts against everything above: the busiest of these shops are throughput operations. Eleven thousand reviews against a nine-to-nine, seven-day week is an enormous number of chairs turning over, and throughput is the natural enemy of the one service you genuinely cannot hurry. Detailed hand-painted work is billed per nail and delivered by patience. If that is what you want, the eleven-thousand-review door is the wrong door, and nine in the morning at a quiet shop is the right one.

If what you want is a clean 200K gel colour, a heel worked by someone who has done four thousand of them, and a bill that arrives without surprises · then the inland shops with the long Korean names are, on the evidence in this dataset, the most reliable bet in Da Nang. Sixty-nine of the 149 salons here are in Hải Châu. Fifteen of the twenty-six are too. Those two facts are not unrelated, and neither of them has anything to do with the beach.

Frequently asked

Are there Korean nail salons in Da Nang?

Yes · 26 of the 149 salons in this guide carry Korean script in their own trading name, more than every other foreign alphabet in the set combined. Fifteen are in Hải Châu, five of those on Trần Phú, and five are on the My Khe beachfront. The signage tells you who the customer is rather than who owns the shop: some are Korean-owned and most appear to be Vietnamese businesses signed for Korean visitors. Three publish a KakaoTalk channel instead of a website.

Where do Korean tourists get their nails done in Da Nang?

Inland, mostly. South Korea was Da Nang's largest source market in July 2026 at 18.63% of inbound visitors, ahead of China at 11.23%, and the salons signed for that market cluster in Hải Châu rather than on the beachfront · fifteen of the 26 in this guide, with five of them on the riverside stretch of Trần Phú. That also puts them on the cheaper side of the beach markup, which runs 10–30% over the city rate for identical work.

Can I get Korean-style nails in Da Nang, and what do they cost?

Yes, and the current Korean techniques · syrup or jelly nails, magnetic line and barcode nails, the blurred nude gradient · are recognised on sight in the Korean-facing shops. Pricing favours you: cat-eye and chrome are billed as a flat full set at around 180K and ombré or French at around 220K, against 200K for plain gel colour, so magnetic and chrome finishes are among the cheapest current looks available. Hand-painted detail is billed per nail at 15K to 100K and is where a bill grows. Bring a reference photo rather than a description.

Is a Da Nang nail salon with thousands of Google reviews better?

Better at some things. Only nineteen businesses in this guide clear a thousand reviews and six of those are Korean-signed; as a group they hold a median 355 reviews against 99 for the rest, at a marginally lower average rating of 4.86 against 4.92. High volume means a standardised, well-drilled service and a technician who has done your treatment thousands of times. It also means turnover, which is the wrong environment for detailed per-nail art. Book the busy shop for gel colour and pedicures, a quiet morning slot for anything hand-painted.
