Prices · 7 min · 27 October 2026

Báo giá: 69K to 1,200,000, and the million-đồng bill where every line was in range

Forty-six of the 715 reviews behind this guide name a sum of money, and 37 of those are nail bills. They run from 69K in Cẩm Lệ to 1,200,000đ on An Thượng 39. The itemised million-đồng bill at DzonDzon has six lines on it, and not one falls outside the published city ranges.

In short
  • Thirty-seven of the 715 reviews behind this guide say what nail work actually cost. The floor is Mỹ Duyên Nail in Cẩm Lệ, at around 69K a combo; the ceiling is 1,200,000đ, about 45 dollars, at Liti Nail & Hair 2 on An Thượng 39. Nineteen of the thirty-seven come to 500K or more. These are bills rather than menu lines, and the published city ranges put gel colour at 70K to 300K and a pedicure with gel at 200K to 300K, because nobody buys one line.
  • The stars do not track the money. Just nine of the forty-six reviews that name a figure fall below five stars, and fourteen of the seventeen bills over 600K are five-star. The customer who paid 1,200,000 calls it very appropriate for what she got. Meanwhile the smallest bill anybody complained about, 180K at HM Nail on Đặng Thai Mai, drew the objection that she had expected to pay half of it.
  • The bill worth studying comes from DzonDzon Nails on 2 Tháng 9, since a customer itemised hers: 50K manicure, 300K extensions, 200K gel colour, 200K art, then 50K and 200K again on the feet. One million đồng and two stars, yet the same paragraph praises the technician's work. Hold those six numbers up to the city ranges and each one falls inside. The stack cost a million. No single line did.
  • Displaying prices is not optional in Vietnam. Decree 87/2024, in force since 12 July 2024, fines a business 500,000 to 1,000,000đ for not displaying its prices or displaying them so unclearly that customers are misled, and 5 to 10 million for charging above the displayed price. Six salons in this city publish that menu online. For everybody else it lives on a wall you have not seen yet, which is why the number has to come out of a message thread before you ride over.

Thirty-seven bills, and both ends of the city

Search the 715 public reviews behind this guide for a sum of money and 46 come back. Four of those are enthusiasm rather than money: two readers rating a salon 1000%, one at 10000 points, one at 10000000 out of ten. One is a daily step count. One is a 100 VND typo. Three more are a massage, a shampoo and a heel scrub rather than nails. That leaves 37 reviews, across a dataset of 151 nail salons and 53,433 Google ratings, that state what somebody paid to have their nails done in this city.

The bottom of the set lies out in the wards nobody flies in for. At Mỹ Duyên Nail, 51 Nguyễn Văn Huyên in Cẩm Lệ, a combo is described at roughly 69K. Lyn Nail Pmu on Hoàng Dư Khương, also in Cẩm Lệ, charges 120K to 160K for a hand-drawn design. Cheap work is not confined to the edges, though. A single-colour gel manicure at Chuty Nailroom, in the lane at 169/5 Trần Phú, came to 190,000đ, while RiKa Nail Spa on An Nhơn 6, three streets from the sand, did a gel manicure and pedicure together for 190K.

Four bills in the set clear a million. Liti Nail & Hair 2 on An Thượng 39: 1,200,000đ, which the customer converts to about 45 dollars and calls appropriate. Lena at 34 An Thượng 3: lines of 400K, 120K and 100K on the hands and 400K and 120K on the feet, three hours in the chair, 1,140,000đ when you add them up. DzonDzon on 2 Tháng 9: a flat million, itemised. Amou Nail studio on Nguyễn Chí Thanh: around 1.35 million for two people, tips excluded. All four of those reviews were posted within the past three months. The September piece on how you pay counted 45 bills in this corpus and put the ceiling at 850K; this is a later snapshot, and the top of the range has moved since. Dates on the data exist for exactly that reason.

The spread does not follow the postcode. Five of the seventeen bills at 600K or more are nowhere near the tourist quarter: 750K at P.nailstudio on Lý Thái Tông in Thanh Khê, 600,000đ for hands and feet at Chic nails on Nguyễn Xí, 700,000đ at Shin Nail in Hòa Cường, and the DzonDzon and Amou bills in central Hải Châu. Lena at 89 Phạm Tu, by contrast, a few minutes from My Khe, did a first-ever manicure and pedicure in two-tone gel for 290,000đ. Rent still shows up in the menu, yet beside what you order it is a rounding error.

The complaint is never about the biggest number

Only nine of the 46 money reviews fall short of five stars, and fourteen of the seventeen bills above 600K carry the full five. At Liti on Đình Nghệ a British customer paid 800,000đ, converted it to £25 and used the word bargain. At Nail Sweet on An Thượng 3 another paid 800K for a removal, a set with art and a pedicure, called it 45 Australian dollars and called that reasonable. Nobody in this dataset has ever been upset by a large number they saw coming.

Now the ones who were upset. Shin Nail, 17 Hồ Biểu Chánh: four stars, and the line that carries the review is that she should have asked about the price beforehand, because she had no idea it would be 700,000đ. She praises the technician for explaining every step as she went. HM Nail on Đặng Thai Mai: three stars, 180K, double what she says she would pay elsewhere, in a review that is mostly about an inexperienced technician she still describes as sweet. And at CASA NAIL SPA, 42 Mai Thúc Lân, a one-star that goes out of its way to say the price menu was perfectly clear.

The pattern shows most cleanly in a review that is not even about money. A regular at Trà Beauty on Trần Cao Vân wrote a very long one-star about air bubbles under a set of box nails. Buried deep in it, she mentions the shop had nudged its prices up with Tết close, calls the extra negligible and acceptable, and asks only for advance notice: announce a 10% Tết surcharge, she suggests, before people book. The surcharge was fine; the silence about it was not.

This piece rests on one opinion. None of the price complaints in these 715 reviews argues that the work was not worth the money. Each of them says nobody told them the number. A quote is not a courtesy good salons hand out. It is the part of the service you notice only when it is missing, three hours in, with wet nails and a cash-only counter in front of you.

Where a million đồng actually goes

At DzonDzon, the customer wanted a full set of gel extensions, a French tip, and one painted flower on a single nail of each hand. She published the bill like this: 50K for the manicure, 300K for the extensions, 200K for the gel colour, 200K for the art, then 50K for the pedicure and another 200K for gel colour on the toes. One million đồng in all, and the part most people would call the manicure was 50K, five per cent of the total.

Set each line beside the ranges computed from the six Da Nang menus that are public. Across the five menus that list extensions, they run 60K to 550K, which makes 300K mid-table. Gel colour spans 70K to 300K across six, so 200K is ordinary, and it is billed twice because it went on twice. Nail art runs 5K to 120K per nail on four menus, and 200K over two painted nails works out to 100K each. Every number on that bill can be defended. Still, the bill is a million, and that is the lesson: the line items are not where the money is, the count of them is.

The pattern repeats. Lena on An Thượng 3: 400K builder infill, 120K chrome, 100K extra for cat eye, then 400K for the pedicure and 120K for chrome again, since chrome went on the toes too. A customer at 1998.nailsroom, 172/5 Trần Phú, lists design, gel removal, chrome, pedicure gel and a second removal reaching 750,000đ altogether, and calls it exceptional value, which it probably is. She also mentions that the salon asked her for the review, a disclosure most of the five-star text in this city does not bother with.

And the single largest line anywhere in this dataset is not nails at all. At R Nail & Spa 1, 1 An Thượng 4, a customer's ombré French came bundled with a 750K package of heel scrub, exfoliation and foot mask; her husband's plain manicure was 80K, and the pair of them left at 830K. The CASA bill has the same shape: a 250K foot-care package billed as five things including a 45-minute massage, which by her account delivered a soak, her heels rubbed, and about eight minutes of massage. The feet are where the add-ons live, and they are priced like a spa because that is what they are.

Niêm yết giá: the wall has legal weight

Vietnam's Decree 87/2024/NĐ-CP, which took effect on 12 July 2024, sets the penalties for price-management offences. Article 13 fines a trader 500,000 to 1,000,000đ for failing to display prices, or for displaying them in a way unclear enough to confuse customers, and 5 to 10 million đồng for selling above the displayed price where the business sets that price itself. The figures double for a company rather than an individual. A price list on the wall of a nail shop is not decoration. It is the document the law thinks you are buying from.

The obligation, though, is to display at the point of sale, not online. Six Da Nang salons put a menu on the web: LENA, Liti, Sunam, Amor Spa, Nail Bơ and Reborn, five of them among the 151 ranked in this guide. Those six menus form the entire basis of the city ranges on this site, last checked on 2 October 2026. Everybody else keeps prices exactly where the law requires and nowhere else, so anyone comparing two salons from a hotel room ends up comparing two Instagram grids.

That is why the CASA review is worth reading twice. She never argued that the menu was hidden or wrong; she says it was clear. Her complaint was being billed for work that did not happen, close to 500,000đ of it by her account, and being told, when she raised it, that it was not expensive for her so she could simply pay. Decree 87 covers charging above a displayed price, but says nothing about a 45-minute massage that lasted eight. That argument plays out at the counter, in cash, with no card behind you to dispute it with afterwards.

The cheap defence is a photograph. Read the menu when you sit down and photograph it, the way you would photograph a hotel room before unpacking. It costs nothing, nobody minds, and it converts the conversation at the end from what each of you remembers into something you can both point at.

Báo giá: getting the number before you ride over

Eleven of the 715 reviews mention a price being asked for in advance, or not. Two of them are at Mom Nails.danang, 157 Nguyễn Chí Thanh. One is a single sentence: she was quoted the price beforehand and will come back. The other says the owner gave a quote before starting, with limited English on both sides and a reference photo doing the rest. At MK Nails Beauty, 15 An Hải Đông 1, the mechanism is the last line of a five-star about a 380K cat eye and chrome set: you can just WhatsApp the shop and ask about price and availability.

You can see what a quote buys in the review of a truly difficult appointment. A customer at Hehe Nail Room, 182b Phan Châu Trinh, was quoted about 750K for removal plus a new set of extensions; she arrived at half twelve for a one o'clock slot and left at half five. Five hours. Her rating is four stars, she says the nails match the photo exactly, and the money never comes up as a grievance, since it had been settled already. Shin Nail's four stars were the mirror image: good work, no number.

The message that prevents all of this runs four lines. List the services in the order they will happen: removal, then hands, then feet. Say how many nails you want painted and ask the per-nail price for art. Whether gel colour on the toes is billed apart from the pedicure is worth asking too, since on the DzonDzon bill it was its own 200K. Then request one total. Keep the reply; 24 of the 715 reviews name the channel they booked through, nearly all of them Instagram, WhatsApp or Messenger, so the quote arrives as text by default.

Two phrases will carry it once the English runs out. Bao nhiêu tiền? means how much. Tổng cộng bao nhiêu? means how much altogether, and that is the one that counts, since an answer to the first usually covers about one line. When the reply is a range, as it will be for art, note the top of it and plan to pay that.

The chair is where the stack grows

Nobody books a million-đồng appointment. That DzonDzon bill is six separate decisions and at least four of them were taken in the chair, hands occupied, two hours in. Sequence is the trick: by the time somebody suggests chrome on your toes, you have already spent the afternoon there and another 120K reads as nothing. The Lena bill on An Thượng 3 is that exact shape, chrome on the hands and then chrome again on the feet, and that customer was pleased with every part of it. This is not a trap. It is just how a bill grows when every step is agreed separately and the total is agreed never.

The rule I follow, every time: when a technician suggests something extra, ask its price before nodding, then say the running total out loud. It takes four seconds and works through a translation app. The surprise also moves from the counter to the chair, where you can still say no thank you.

Should the final total still come in above the agreed number, the response is unglamorous. Show the message and ask which line accounts for the difference. Then have them point to it on the wall. A rounding of 20K or 50K is not worth your afternoon; a 500,000 gap is. Keep the scale in view, too: two reviews in 715 allege being charged more than they were told, one at CASA and one at Timi Nail on Lê Đình Dương, where a customer says the figure doubled overnight. Two in 715 does not make a city of hustlers, just a city that does not volunteer totals.

When a shop will not give you a number before starting, it is usually not hiding a scam. What it tells you, accurately, is that the total depends on how the appointment goes, and that is exactly the problem. Ask anyway. Of the six or seven things worth checking at the door, this is the one needing only a single message, and a salon that answers in writing has already told you how the afternoon will end.

Frequently asked

Do nail salons in Da Nang overcharge tourists?

Rarely, if you mean a made-up price; often, if you mean an unstated total. Among the 715 public reviews behind this guide, two allege being charged more than they were told. Much more common is a bill that is correct line by line and far bigger than expected. The itemised one-million-đồng bill at DzonDzon on 2 Tháng 9 has six lines, and each sits inside the ranges published by the six Da Nang salons with public menus. Most of this disappears if you ask for one total before any work starts.

How much does a manicure and pedicure cost in Da Nang?

Bills named in public reviews range from around 69,000đ for a combo in Cẩm Lệ to 1,200,000đ on An Thượng 39, and nineteen of the thirty-seven reported bills reach 500,000đ or more. Plain gel colour on hands and feet, nothing added, appears at about 190,000 to 400,000đ. Across the six salons that publish prices, the menu lines are narrower: 70K to 300K for gel colour and 200K to 300K for a pedicure with gel.

How do I get a price quote from a Da Nang nail salon before my appointment?

Send the salon a message on Instagram, WhatsApp or Messenger; 24 of the 715 reviews here say that is how they booked. Give the services in the order they will happen (removal, hands, feet) and say how many nails you want painted. Ask what nail art costs per nail and whether gel on the toes is billed separately, then request one total. Save the reply on your phone. Tổng cộng bao nhiêu? means how much altogether.

Why was my nail art more expensive than the manicure?

Because art is charged per nail. At the four Da Nang salons that publish that line, it costs 5,000 to 120,000đ per nail, so ten nails of detailed work can run to several times the manicure beneath it. The DzonDzon bill is the clearest published case: 200,000đ of art on two nails beside a 50,000đ manicure. Settle the per-nail figure and the number of nails before the first brushstroke.
