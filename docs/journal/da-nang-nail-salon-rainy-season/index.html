650mm in October: booking a Da Nang nail appointment in the wet season

Three of the 715 reviews behind this guide mention rain, and every one of them ends with a salon handing something over · an umbrella, a raincoat, tea while it passed. Not one of the 149 salons sits inside a mall. From September that matters.

In short
  • The wet season opens now and runs to December. Climatestotravel's Da Nang series puts September at 310mm over 14 rain days, October at 650mm over 20, November at 430mm over 20. September is an interruption. October is a planning constraint.
  • There is nowhere indoors to wait it out. Not one of the 149 salons in this guide is inside a mall or a hotel, and exactly one is upstairs · Tầng 2, 178 Bạch Đằng. Everything else is a street-level shopfront you arrive at wet.
  • Density is the thing that saves you. Median distance from a salon to its nearest competitor is 68m on the My Khe beachfront, 78m in Hải Châu, 85m in An Thượng. In a downpour, take the near door and run the ninety-second check when you are inside it.
  • Move the toe work. Corner-cutting and street water in the same 48 hours is the one genuinely bad idea in this article · and on 28 October 2025 Ngũ Hành Sơn, the ward on 35 of these salons' addresses, was among the ones under water.

Three reviews in 715 mention rain. All three end with somebody handing something over.

Search the 715 public reviews behind this guide for weather and almost nothing comes back. Three reviews use the word rain. One uses umbrella. Nobody, in the entire set, has written the words storm, typhoon, flood or monsoon. That is not a claim about Da Nang's climate · it is a claim about who writes English reviews of nail salons, and when they were here.

The three that exist are worth reading together, because they say the same thing three times. At a beachfront studio on the My Khe strip, a reviewer finishes a five-star write-up about the price and the design work by noting, almost as an afterthought, that it was raining and they were given an umbrella. At a Korean-signed spa a street back, a customer describes being served tea and snacks while waiting for a shower to stop. At a studio in the An Thượng alleys, a reviewer who had come for nails and lashes mentions that one of the technicians lent her a raincoat so she could get to an ATM, because the shop takes cash only.

None of those is a review of the manicure. All three are descriptions of a shop treating a downpour as its problem rather than yours, and that is the wet-season insight in this whole piece. From September to December, a nail appointment is the cheapest two-hour indoor block in this city: 200K for a gel colour on the price tables, no booking strictly required, and the service gets more attentive the emptier the room is.

Not one salon in a mall. One upstairs. Everything else is a shutter on a street.

Run the 149 businesses in this guide against every word that would signal covered access · Vincom, Lotte, plaza, mall, hotel, resort · and you get nothing. Zero hits. One single salon in the set publishes a floor above ground level, Moon Spa at Tầng 2, 178 Bạch Đằng on the riverside. The other 148 are ground-floor shopfronts behind a roll-up shutter, which is the normal shape of Vietnamese retail and a genuinely different proposition from the mall-floor nail counters you get in Bangkok or Singapore.

So the wet part of the appointment is the last hundred metres, and where you book decides how bad it is. Six salons here are addressed on Võ Nguyên Giáp, the beachfront boulevard · a wide road with a long, exposed pavement between wherever a Grab can stop and wherever you are going. Twelve are on Trần Phú, which has the same geometry inland. The An Thượng grid is the opposite: the addresses there are alley numbers · An Thượng 3, 29, 32, 34 · lanes narrow enough that a driver puts you down effectively at the door.

That is the one piece of routing advice I would give for the wet half of the year. In a settled dry week the beachfront markup buys you a five-minute walk from the sand and that is a fair trade. In October it buys you a five-minute walk in 650 millimetres' worth of weather. Book the alley.

310 in September, 650 in October, 430 in November

The numbers are not close to each other, which is why treating the wet season as one thing gets people in trouble. Climatestotravel's Da Nang page gives 310mm across 14 rain days for September, 650mm across 20 for October, 430mm across 20 for November and 215mm across 18 for December, and puts the tropical cyclone window at early September to 10 December. Fourteen rain days in September means half the month is dry and the rain that falls tends to arrive as a short late-afternoon collapse rather than a grey day.

October is a different animal, and last year is the reference case rather than a hypothetical. On 28 October 2025 Da Nang declared a state of emergency covering transport infrastructure after consecutive days of rain; VietnamPlus reported more than 65,000 households affected, over 1,400 households and 5,600 residents evacuated, and nearly 1,900 metres of road destroyed across the city. Ngũ Hành Sơn was named among the submerged wards. Two days later the Thu Bồn, down in what is now the Hội An end of the enlarged city, peaked at 5.62m · 14 centimetres above the 1964 benchmark, per reporting on the flood.

Thirty-five of the 149 salons in this guide carry Ngũ Hành Sơn in their published address, which is the ward that swallowed Mỹ An in the 2025 reshuffle. That is not a reason to avoid them and I want to be clear about it, because a once-in-sixty-years river peak is not a forecast. It is a reason to keep a late-October appointment loose: message the shop the morning of, rather than treating a booking made four days earlier as fixed.

Book the nine o'clock slot, not the four o'clock one

The trade here opens early and the data is unambiguous about it. Of the 145 salons publishing hours, 69 open at nine, 25 at eight and 24 at half past eight · so 118 of them are working before nine thirty. At the other end, 28 close at eight, 22 at half past and 22 at nine, which is the evening halving covered elsewhere in this journal.

Set that against when the rain actually turns up. Every travel guide describing a Da Nang September says the same thing in slightly different words: short bursts, usually late afternoon or evening, rarely more than an hour. So the morning is not merely convenient in the wet season, it is structurally the dry part of the day, and the appointment is long enough that the difference matters. Reviewers in this dataset describe a complicated art set taking two and a half hours and a two-technician mani-pedi with extensions running about three.

Do the arithmetic once and it books itself. A nine o'clock start on a gel colour and a pedicure puts you back on the street around eleven, before the afternoon cell has built. A four o'clock start on the same pair of services puts you outside a shutter at six, in the dark, in the exact ninety minutes the sky has been aiming at all day.

The pedicure is the appointment to move · not cancel, move

Here is the one hard rule. Water standing in a Da Nang street after a proper downpour is not rainwater, it is drainage, and you do not want it meeting skin that was opened this morning. That rules out one specific step rather than the whole treatment.

Lấy khóe · the dig into the corner of the toenail · is already the step this guide tells you to refuse year-round, on the strength of Vinmec's published warning about swelling, pus and, in severe cases, tissue death. In the wet season the case against it stops being a probability argument and becomes a scheduling one. A broken nail fold, a warm enclosed shoe and a wade through street water is the full recipe for paronychia, and you will be back on a plane before it declares itself.

What survives the rain perfectly well is everything that does not break skin. Heel and callus work · 250K for the 40-minute express ritual, 590K for the 75-minute signature on the pedicure tables · touches hardened skin with no nerve endings and no pocket for bacteria to sit in. Gel colour on toes at 180K is cured under a lamp and is waterproof before you stand up. Take those two, decline the corners, and the wet season costs you nothing at all.

And if you are here for the last week of October specifically, front-load the feet. Get the pedicure on the first dry morning of the trip rather than saving it for the day before you fly, because the day before you fly is the day you have no flexibility left.

In a downpour, take the nearest door

The thing that makes all of the above survivable is how tightly packed this trade is. Measure every salon in the set to its closest competitor and the median distances are 68m on the My Khe beachfront, 78m in Hải Châu and 85m in My An and An Thượng. Stand at the An Thượng 29 junction and there are 12 salons inside 300 metres, 28 inside 500. You are essentially never more than a minute's sprint from a nail shop in this city.

Which changes the decision. In dry weather it is worth walking past three shops to reach the one you researched. In a wet-season downpour that is a bad trade, and the correct move is the near door plus thirty seconds of judgement once you are through it: files and buffers unwrapped in front of you, a steriliser with its light on, a written menu with removal priced on it. That is the whole check, and it does not care which shop you meant to visit.

Walk-ins back this up. Fifteen of the 715 reviews describe arriving with no appointment and being seated immediately, several of them noting the shop was empty at the time. Wet-season afternoons are when that is most true, and it is the closest thing to an off-season perk you will find here · nobody advertises rainy-day rates, and I have not seen a single posted menu that changes between June and November.

One errand to do before you sit down, though: get the cash. Four reviews in this set mention cash outright, one describing an owner walking to two banks to work out how a foreign card could withdraw, another the raincoat lent for an ATM run. Card acceptance is patchy and the machine is always outside. In the dry months that is a footnote. Between now and December it is the only part of the appointment you cannot do under a roof.

Frequently asked

When is the rainy season in Da Nang?

September to December, with the tropical cyclone window running from early September to about 10 December. Climatestotravel's Da Nang figures are 310mm of rain across 14 rain days in September, 650mm across 20 days in October, 430mm across 20 in November and 215mm across 18 in December. October is by a wide margin the wettest month and the one where flooding is a real trip variable rather than an inconvenience · on 28 October 2025 Da Nang declared a transport state of emergency, with more than 65,000 households affected.

Can you still get your nails done in Da Nang when it rains?

Yes, and it is arguably the best thing to do with a wet afternoon here. Salons open early · 118 of the 145 that publish hours are working by half past nine · and a gel colour runs about 200K for roughly two hours indoors. The complication is not the appointment, it is getting to it: none of the 149 salons in this guide is inside a mall or a hotel, so every one of them is a street-level shopfront with an exposed last hundred metres. Book somewhere in the An Thượng alleys rather than on the beachfront boulevard and that walk mostly disappears.

Will rain ruin a fresh manicure in Da Nang?

Not if it is gel. Gel is cured hard under a UV lamp before you leave the chair, so it is fully waterproof the moment you stand up · same reason it survives the sea. Classic polish at around 100K is the one to avoid in the wet season, because it needs real air-drying time and a rainy scooter ride ten minutes after your appointment will print the weather into it. If you are booking during a wet spell, spend the extra hundred thousand đồng on gel.

Should I get a pedicure in Da Nang during the rainy season?

Have the pedicure, decline lấy khóe · the step where the technician digs into the corner of the toenail. Cut skin inside a closed shoe plus a wade through flooded street drainage is how paronychia starts, and Vinmec publishes a warning about corner-cutting leading to swelling, pus and in severe cases tissue death. Everything that does not break skin is fine: heel and callus work from 250K, gel colour on toes at 180K, cured and waterproof before you leave. Book it for a dry morning early in the trip rather than the day before your flight.
