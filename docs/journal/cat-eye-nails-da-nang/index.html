Mắt mèo: 80K where cat eye counts as a colour, 180K where it counts as art

Cat eye is named in 24 of the 715 reviews behind this guide, at 20 of the 151 salons, which is more than French work and nearly three times chrome. Not one of those 24 reviews mentions a magnet, and the magnet is the whole trick. Two Da Nang menus publish a price for it, and they disagree about what kind of thing it is.

In short
  • In this city, cat eye is the finish people ask for. Of the 715 public reviews behind this guide, 51 name a finish of any kind, and 24 of those 51 name cat eye, across 20 of the 151 salons. French work follows with 16 reviews, chrome with nine, then matte six, ombré five, jelly two and glitter one. Seven of the 20 cat-eye salons sit on the My Khe beachfront and six in Hải Châu.
  • Two salons with published menus behind the price table give it a number, and they put it on opposite sides of the same line. Nail Bơ at 122A Bắc Sơn lists sơn mắt mèo at 80K for all ten nails (10K above its 70K plain gel) and places tráng gương, the mirror finish, under design at 5K to 10K per nail. Reborn Nails & Retreat on Châu Thị Vĩnh Tế prints cat eye or chrome as a single full-set line at 180K and ombré or French as a second at 220K.
  • What moves your bill is less which effect you want than how the shop files it: as the colour itself, or as an extra on top of it. One customer at Lena's An Thượng 3 room itemised hers: 400K builder infill, 120K chrome, 100K more for the cat eye, then a 400K pedicure plus another 120K of chrome. A cat-eye manicure alone at Suzy Nails on 109 Trần Phú came to 300K.
  • This piece's view: on a short trip, buy the effect and not the painting. Twenty-four reviews, twenty salons, and not one magnet

Search the 715 reviews behind this guide for a finish instead of a colour and 51 turn up, spread across 40 of the 151 salons. Of those, cat eye takes 24, written four different ways, at 20 separate addresses. Next comes French work with 16 reviews at 15 salons; chrome gets nine at six, then matte six, ombré five, jelly two and glitter one. About half of what this city's English-speaking customers say about the top of a nail concerns one effect.

Since none of those 24 reviews says what it is: cat-eye gel holds microscopic metal flakes in suspension. After painting the nail, the technician holds a magnet over the wet surface for a second or two, and the flakes gather into a band of light that slides as your hand turns. That is how Vietnamese nail-supply sites describe it. naillamos.com has a trade explainer on the types sold now, while analia.vn, which distributes the ANSC line here, lists its cat-eye pots at 80,000 đồng each, with cheaper marketplace jars from about 35,000. Keep that figure in mind. Pigment for a whole hand costs roughly what one Da Nang salon charges to put it on.

Nobody in the dataset writes mắt mèo, the Vietnamese name, and nobody writes magnet. The method is invisible from the chair, so customers describe the outcome instead: a red-chocolate cat eye at CHUBBY NAILS, 73 Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai, a neutral pink cat-eye GelX set at M Nail & Spa on Võ Nguyên Giáp, cat eye mixed with a little glitter at EIRA NAILS, 117 Hùng Vương. One customer at Lyn Nail Pmu, 72 Hoàng Dư Khương out in Cẩm Lệ, had it on her feet, liked it enough to come back for her hands, and had a set done in Hanoi taken off to make room.

The map is lopsided. Seven of the 20 salons are on the My Khe beachfront, where this guide counts 32 nail salons altogether, and six are in Hải Châu, home to 64. My An and An Thượng have three, Sơn Trà two, Thanh Khê one, and one is further out. On the beach streets the request comes up at better than twice the rate of the centre, roughly what you would expect from a finish that photographs well and adds a minute to the job.

Colour or design: the line on the menu that decides the bill

Nail Bơ, at 122A Bắc Sơn in An Khê, publishes the most legible price list in this trade, and it ends with a footnote that does more work than the numbers: the figures are per nail in the design column and per set of ten in the gel column. The gel column has two lines. Sơn gel, plain gel colour, 70K, with 10K on top if you want three colours or more mixed. Sơn mắt mèo, 80K. Ten nails, finished. The effect costs ten thousand đồng.

Next is the design column, where everything is counted one finger at a time: a simple or raised painted motif 5K to 10K, ấn xà cừ or ấn nhũ (pressed shell, pressed pigment) 10K to 15K, tráng gương, which is what English calls chrome, 5K to 10K, a French tip on one nail 6K, stickers 5K to 10K, stones or charms 5K to 20K, sculpted gel 10K to 20K. Times ten, a full French at this shop comes to 60K and a full chrome set to 50K to 100K. By comparison, its cuticle work is 20K, its gel removal 10K and its set of soft-gel tips 60K.

Reborn Nails & Retreat, 56 Châu Thị Vĩnh Tế in My An, puts the line somewhere else. Its printed menu, as of 2 October 2026, gives cat eye and chrome one shared line at 180K for the full set, under nail art. A second full-set line, ombré or French, is 220K. Hand-painted design runs 15K to 100K per nail and 3D chrome art 40K to 80K per nail; pearls, charms and glitter are 20K to 80K, stickers 10K to 40K. Gel colour there costs 200K.

Set the two menus beside each other and the same two effects land on opposite sides of the same accounting line. Out at Bắc Sơn, cat eye is a colour you pick instead of another colour and chrome is a decoration counted per nail. In My An, cat eye and chrome are per-set effects and French joins them. Neither shop is wrong and neither is hiding anything. What it means is that the phrase cat-eye manicure has no fixed price in this city, and the only question that moves the number is whether the effect replaces the gel colour line or sits on top of it. Ask it in those words, because the art column is where Da Nang bills grow: the city range is 5K to 120K per nail across the four published menus that carry it, against 70K to 300K for a gel colour across six.

What people actually paid

A customer at Lena's An Thượng 3 room has the clearest bill in the dataset, and she wrote out every line: 400K for the builder BIAB infill, 120K for chrome, an extra 100K for the cat eye, then 400K for the pedicure plus another 120K of chrome on the toes. She spent three hours in the salon and says plainly that this is not the quick option. Here the add-on model is out in the open. Across hands and feet the effects alone came to 340K, which is most of what the infill itself cost.

Other figures sit lower, since they cover less. A reviewer at Chic nails, 68 Nguyễn Xí in Thanh Khê paid 360K for a removal, a new cat eye and a design on two nails, booked through WhatsApp, and calls it great value. At MK Nails Beauty, 15 An Hải Đông 1, 380K covered a sunset ombré using both cat eye and chrome; she advises messaging the shop about price and availability first. And at CIAO NAIL STUDIO 2, 05 Nguyễn Thông, an aunt's cat-eye manicure and pedicure came to 600K together, on a visit where the niece's extension set with a complex design and charms was 850K.

At the top of the review counts, Suzy Nails at 109 Trần Phú, which with 10,343 reviews is the most-reviewed nail salon in this guide, charged 300K for a cat-eye manicure and took 50 minutes over it.

So: 80K published at one end, 600K reported at the other, for a band of light drawn by the same magnet. Those are single bills and each covers different work, which is the reason to compare the per-set line rather than the total. Removal, a base, the shaping and sometimes a second technician are all in there too. The plain gel manicure is the figure to anchor everything else to.

The magnet is the cheap part, the mixing is not

A customer at Liti Nail Hair My Khe Beach, 107A Đình Nghệ (1,218 reviews at 4.9) came in with stained-glass windows as her reference. Huong, the technician she names, custom-mixed the cat-eye gels to reach the colours in the photo, and the reviewer judges the line work finer than the Pinterest original. A detail in the same review is worth more than the compliment: her nails react to acetone, so her old soft gel came off with a sanding bit alone.

The custom mix is the tell. Cat-eye gel arrives in pots of fixed colour, so matching a photograph means blending them, and blending them means somebody who knows what the magnet will do to the blend afterwards. That is the part you are paying a skilled room for, not the second with the magnet in the air.

It also stacks, which is why it turns up inside so many bills. At MOON NAILS, 48 Đặng Vũ Hỷ (282 reviews at a flat five), one set carried cat eye, shimmer, chrome and ombré at once. At The C Nails, 260 Phan Châu Trinh, a visitor had GelX extensions with cat eye and 3D work on the hands and cat eye again on the toes. At Bảo-Hân Nail Bar, 08 Phan Thành Tài, it was gel with French tips and a chrome overlay, done on a Sunday morning in an empty shop, and the customer mentions being handed gloves for the lamp, which she had never been given in Singapore.

Here is where I would put a holiday budget. An effect priced per set that matches the photo on your phone is better value than ten nails of hand-painting that do not, and at 5K to 120K a nail the painting is the line that quietly doubles a total. The photo is doing most of the talking anyway, so bring one that shows the band of light rather than a flat colour, because a cat eye is impossible to describe and obvious to point at.

Both unhappy reviews are about the bill, and one of them is a toe

Twenty-two of the 24 cat-eye reviews are five stars. The two that are not have nothing to say against the finish.

The Suzy Nails customer had the 300K cat-eye manicure on a Tuesday, thought the service good, and went out of her way to change money so she could tip the technician. She came back the next day for a 300K pedicure. This time she was told her toenails were too small for cat eye and she could have a solid colour only, although the day before the same shop had offered to mix colours for her. The pedicure took 25 minutes against the previous day's 50, she says the result looks like ordinary polish, and when she raised it she was told that pedicures naturally take less time. No refund was discussed. She deleted her own five-star review from the day before and wrote this one instead. Notice what she is not complaining about: the hands were good.

The second review is from CASA NAIL SPA, 42 Mai Thúc Lân (326 reviews at 4.6). Its opening line is first time getting cat eye nails, and I love them; it scores the service itself eight out of ten, then becomes nearly 500,000 đồng of dispute over a 250K foot-care package whose 45-minute massage she timed at about eight. The useful part is her own advice: get the prices up front and be specific about what you are going to be charged for.

As for the toes, physics was not behind the refusal at Trần Phú. SuNam Nail & Spa at 12 Phan Bôi (629 reviews) put cat eye on a customer's hands and toes and she was delighted, The C Nails did a cat-eye pedicure, and the Lyn Nail customer out in Cẩm Lệ had her feet done first. A toenail does leave the flakes less room to form a band, and a little toe leaves them almost none, so a technician who steers you to a solid colour on the feet may be giving you the better result. In that review the problem lies not with the advice but with its arrival after the money.

My own order, then: cat eye on the hands, plain gel on the toes. The toes get done first and then spend the week in sand, nobody is studying the shimmer on a fourth toe, and an effect that costs 80K to 180K a set is worth buying where you can watch it move.

What to say before the magnet comes out

There are four things, and one message holds them all. Give the effect's name in both languages: cat eye and mắt mèo will each land, and tráng gương gets you chrome faster than the English word does. State how many nails, because a shop like Nail Bơ prices a design per nail, so two accent nails cost a fifth of ten. Ask whether the effect replaces the gel colour or adds to it; that is this whole piece in one sentence. Finally, request hands and feet as two separate numbers instead of a total, since that is the gap the Trần Phú review fell into.

Booking here happens in a message thread anyway, so none of this costs you a phone call. If you are still picking the shop, the signal to look for is a salon that answers a price question in a sentence, and the two salons in this piece that publish the number did exactly that before anyone walked in.

Frequently asked

What is cat eye nail polish?

A gel with microscopic metal flakes suspended in it. The technician paints the nail and then holds a magnet over the wet surface for a second or two, which pulls the flakes into a bright band that appears to move as your hand turns. In Vietnamese it is sơn mắt mèo, literally cat-eye polish, and Vietnamese nail-supply sites such as naillamos.com and analia.vn sell the pots from about 35,000 đồng for marketplace jars to 80,000 for the ANSC line they distribute. It cures like any other gel and lasts as long, so it is a colour choice rather than a different treatment. Not one of the 715 English-language reviews behind this guide mentions the magnet, which is why most customers have no idea the step exists.

How much does cat eye cost in Da Nang?

Two Da Nang salons publish a price, and the figures sit far apart because each classifies the effect differently. Nail Bơ at 122A Bắc Sơn treats it as a colour and lists sơn mắt mèo at 80K for all ten nails, 10K more than its 70K plain gel colour. Reborn Nails &amp; Retreat at 56 Châu Thị Vĩnh Tế prints cat eye or chrome as a 180K full-set line under nail art, on top of a 200K gel colour. Customers report figures between those and above them: 300K for a cat-eye manicure at Suzy Nails on Trần Phú, 360K at Chic nails for a removal plus cat eye plus a design on two nails, 380K at MK Nails Beauty for an ombré involving cat eye and chrome, and 100K as a stated add-on at Lena's An Thượng 3 room. That single question, whether the effect replaces the gel colour line or is added to it, explains the whole spread, so ask it.

Can you get cat eye nails on your toes in Da Nang?

Yes, and several salons in this guide clearly do. One reviewer at SuNam Nail &amp; Spa, 12 Phan Bôi, had hands and toes done in cat eye; The C Nails on Phan Châu Trinh did a cat-eye pedicure along with a cat-eye manicure; a customer at Lyn Nail Pmu in Cẩm Lệ had her feet done first and then returned for her hands. A single shop refused. At Suzy Nails on Trần Phú a customer was told her toenails were too small for the style and she could have a solid colour only, when she had already paid 300K the previous day for cat eye on her fingers. The refusal does have something real behind it, because a small nail leaves the flakes little room to form a band, but settle the price for hands and feet separately before anyone starts so you are not paying effect money for plain polish.

What is the difference between cat eye and chrome nails?

Cat eye is magnetic: flakes inside the gel are dragged into a line of light, so the nail has a bright stripe that shifts with the angle. Chrome is a pigment powder, tráng gương in Vietnamese, rubbed over a cured surface to give a flat mirror or metallic finish with no moving band. Both take minutes rather than hours, which is why Da Nang menus tend to price them as one line. How they are billed is the thing to check. Reborn Nails &amp; Retreat puts cat eye and chrome together at 180K for the full set. Nail Bơ splits them, 80K for cat eye across ten nails and 5K to 10K per nail for chrome, so a full chrome set there lands between 50K and 100K. They stack, too: one customer at MOON NAILS on Đặng Vũ Hỷ had cat eye, shimmer, chrome and ombré in the same set.
