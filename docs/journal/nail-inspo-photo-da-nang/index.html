Mẫu nail: the photo on your phone does more work than your English

49 of the 715 reviews behind this guide describe showing a technician a picture. Forty-eight are five stars and 31 reach for a word meaning exactly. The one that isn't five stars is about five hours in a chair on Phan Châu Trinh.

In short
  • Bring the picture and stop rehearsing the sentence. 49 of the 715 reviews behind this guide describe showing a technician a photo, across 40 of the 149 salons · 48 are five stars, and 31 of the 49 reach for exactly, identical, perfectly or better than.
  • Send it the night before, not from the chair. A reviewer at a My Khe studio messaged her reference over WhatsApp and walked in to find the design already sampled · booking here runs on messaging apps anyway.
  • The photo is a bill before it is a design. Hand-painted work runs 15–100K per nail and 3D gel flowers 40–70K, so ten fingers of the second is 400–700K sitting on top of a 200K colour. Cat-eye is 180K for the whole set.

Forty-nine photos, forty-eight of them five stars

Search the 715 public reviews behind this guide for people who arrived holding a picture and 49 come back, spread across 40 of the 149 salons. Forty-eight are five stars. The single exception is a four, and it is not about the nails · it is about five hours.

Before that gets oversold: 674 of all 715 reviews here are five stars anyway, so 94% is the house average and 98% is barely a lift. Stars are the wrong instrument. What the photo changes is the vocabulary. Thirty-one of the 49 reach for a word of exact correspondence · exactly, identical, perfectly, better than the photo · and that language is scarce everywhere else in the dataset. People who bring a reference do not write that the nails were lovely. They write that the nails matched.

The 49 are also not clustered where you would guess. Twenty-four come from Hải Châu, the working city centre, against 12 from the My Khe beachfront and nine from the An Thượng grid. That is roughly the shape of the guide as a whole, which is worth knowing: copying a picture is not a tourist-strip specialism you pay a premium for. The studio on Nguyễn Hoàng is doing it, and so is the one-woman room at 12 Tụ Quán.

The mechanism is not translation. A photo is a specification, and it carries five things at once that a sentence carries badly · the colour, the finish, where the design sits on the nail, the length and the shape · in an object both people can point at. Nothing has to be remembered across two hours.

Here is the number that decided this article. Six reviews in the 715 use the phrase language barrier. All six are five stars. Seven name Google Translate. All seven are five stars. A customer at THAO NAILS on Nguyễn Chí Thanh, 1,890 reviews, describes exactly that combination · no shared language, a translation app, an inspiration photo · and ends by saying the result came out better than she had asked for. Whatever the communication problem is in this trade, it is not the thing that ruins appointments.

Send it the night before, not from the chair

At TT95 NAIL on Dương Tự Minh, 166 reviews at a flat five, a customer arranged the whole appointment over WhatsApp and sent her reference ahead. By the time she arrived the artist had already worked the design up as a sample. The reviewer's point is that she knew what she was getting before the first file came out, which is the entire argument for doing it this way.

The same pattern repeats across the better reviews in this set. BIJOU on Tạ Mỹ Duật, 134 reviews, took the booking and the inspiration images in the same WhatsApp thread. THAO NAILS ran it through Facebook Messenger. SORA on Phan Châu Trinh is described as responsive on Instagram. Mây Nailroom, out on Nguyễn Phong Sắc in Cẩm Lệ and a long way from any tourist, handled a foreign customer on WhatsApp with Google Translate doing the rest, and the review notes that the technician assessed the nails and proposed a different service from the one requested.

A photo sent overnight can do three things it cannot do while you are sitting in the chair with your coat still on. It gets checked against what the shop actually stocks in gels, chrome and charms. It gets priced by someone who is not looking at your face while they do the sum. And it can be refused early enough for you to book somewhere else.

The infrastructure supports this whether or not anyone designed it that way. 59 of the 149 salons in this guide publish no website at all; of the 90 that do, 28 point at Instagram and 22 at Facebook. For most of this trade the web address is an inbox, which is why booking here means messaging rather than calling.

One refinement. Send the picture, not a description of the picture. French tips with a bit of gold is a sentence that gets translated twice and arrives as something else. A screenshot is not translated at all.

Your reference photo is a bill of materials

The price tables in this guide price nail art two entirely different ways, and the line between them is the single most expensive thing in your photo. Effects that cover the whole set at once are flat: cat-eye or chrome about 180K, ombré or a French tip about 220K, on top of a 200K gel colour. Everything applied nail by nail multiplies. Stickers 10–40K each, hand-painted design 15–100K each, 3D chrome 40–80K, 3D gel flowers 40–70K, pearls and charms and glitter 20–80K.

Run that over ten fingers. A cat-eye set is 180K and about an hour. Ten hand-painted nails at the top of the band is 1,000K. Both look like a nice manicure on a phone screen at the moment you hold it up, and there is a factor of five between them.

The bills people actually report land in between, and they read exactly as you would expect once you know the rule. RiKa Nail Spa on An Nhơn 6: extensions with gel polish and a simple polka dot, 300K. Hanii on An Cư 1: a full set of extensions with art, 550K. Lalla on An Thượng 29: an extension refill with art plus a full pedicure and foot massage, which the reviewer converted to $44. SORA: 680K on one visit, 750K on another. CIAO's Nguyễn Thông branch: 850K for extensions with gel polish, complex design and charms.

The cleanest illustration is one bill. At Amou Nail studio on Nguyễn Chí Thanh, 279 reviews, a mother and daughter went in together. The daughter's nail art came to 390K, her mother's to 290K, two French pedicures at 290K each and two foot spas at 60K each, about 1.35 million for the pair. Same salon, same afternoon, same hands doing the work. The hundred thousand đồng between the two sets is how many nails had a brush on them.

So read your own reference the way the person quoting it reads it. Count the nails carrying painting, count the charms, and work out whether the effect covers the set in one pass or arrives finger by finger. Learning the four words for the flat-rate finishes · cat-eye, chrome, ombré, French · is worth more than any amount of pointing, because it lets you ask for the 180K version of the picture you brought.

And it is a clock

The same 49 reviews record how long the work took, and the spread is wider than anything on the price side. Hanii on the My Khe beachfront: full set extensions with nail art, one hour ten. Oanh Beauty & Nail out on Trần Duy Chiến in Sơn Trà: an hour and fifteen, including taking off the gel she walked in wearing, with the technician checking in through the whole thing. SORA: two and a half hours, reported independently by two different customers. Posh on An Thượng 9: a complicated design in the same two and a half. Lalla on An Thượng 29: three hours, with two technicians working on her at once.

Then the outlier, and it is the only review in the 49 that is not five stars. Hehe Nail Room at 182b Phan Châu Trinh, 43 reviews at 4.9, booked for 1pm. The customer and her friend turned up early, at half twelve. They left at half five. Five hours. She writes that the nails match the reference, that 750K covering removal and a new set of extensions was worth it, and gives four stars anyway. The whole deduction is the clock. They had budgeted four hours at the outside.

It would be easy to blame the picture for that, so here is the control case. Chuty Nailroom on Trần Phú quoted a customer thirty minutes for a single-colour gel manicure and took two hours. Three stars. No reference photo was involved at any point. Salons in this city under-quote time as a habit, and a detailed picture simply gives the habit more to work with.

The fix costs one extra question. Ask how many hours in the same breath as how many đồng, and treat the answer as a floor rather than an estimate. Then put the complicated set at the start of a day rather than the end · nine to nine is the standard Da Nang salon week, and a five-hour appointment beginning at six in the evening finishes after the shutters should be down, which is not a good position to be negotiating a shape from. If you have a dinner booked, say so when you send the photo. It is the single piece of information most likely to change what gets proposed to you.

The most useful answer your photo can get is no

Pink Nail Studio at 167 Trần Phú carries 2,020 Google reviews at 4.9. A customer walked in without an appointment, was seated straight away, and held up a reference. She was told plainly which parts of it the shop could do and which it could not · no 3D work · and she chose from the salon's own selection of designs instead. Five stars, a note that the range was wide, and a detail that belongs in this article: her technician spoke no English, and two colleagues on the floor translated. The iced tea was free.

At 수지네일 Suzy Nails on Trần Phú, 11,138 reviews and the largest review count anywhere in this guide, a first-time manicure customer describes her technician recreating the design with the materials available. That phrase is the whole trade. Nobody in this city stocks every gel, pigment, powder and charm that exists on Pinterest, and the honest version of copying a picture is a conversation about substitutes.

The good version of yes looks like this. At Liti Nail Hair My Khe Beach on Đình Nghệ, 1,068 reviews, a customer brought stained-glass windows as her reference. The technician custom-mixed cat-eye gels to reach the colours in the photo, and · because the customer said she reacts badly to acetone · took her old soft gel off with a sanding bit instead of a soak. That is not obedience to a picture. That is someone reading it and then telling you what she intends to do differently.

There is an inverse to all of this that foreign customers almost never see. Vietnamese salon guides describe shops posting their mẫu nail · nail designs, literally samples · to their Facebook pages so customers can browse and choose before they come in. The Vietnamese customer picks from the shop's book. The foreign customer arrives with Pinterest. The second is fine; the first is often better, because everything in that book has already been done in that room, with those materials, by those hands.

So here is the flat opinion. A salon that nods at every photo is not being accommodating, it is planning to improvise on your hands for two hours and hope. The three questions worth asking at the door cover removal, what is included and which gel system they use · add a fourth, hold the photo up, and listen for whether any part of the answer is a qualification. The shops worth your hands come back with a question of their own.

What a photo cannot carry

A reference shows a shape on someone else's hands at someone else's length, and this is where sets go quietly wrong. Vietnamese has short words for all of it: móng vuông square, móng tròn round, móng hạnh nhân almond, móng vuông bầu the squared-off oval, plus dài and ngắn for long and short. Harper's Bazaar Việt Nam runs a guide to picking a shape that suits the hand you actually have rather than the one in the picture, which is a distinction no screenshot will ever make for you.

At 1998.nailsroom, 172/5 Trần Phú, 387 reviews at a flat five, a customer who describes herself as fussy about shape says almond is the one most salons miss on the first attempt · and then notes what the studio did without being asked: two coats of base, three of colour. Neither of those is in anybody's reference photo. Both are the reason the reference photo ends up looking like the reference photo.

Filing method is the other silent variable. The Liti reviewer on Đình Nghệ noticed that each nail was shaped with a hand file rather than a machine, at the length she had asked for, and rates the precision above what she gets at home in the UK. Speed and shape are one decision, taken by the technician, in a step that happens before any colour is discussed.

For everything the picture leaves out, use the app. Vietnamese has been in Google Translate's instant camera translation since July 2019, when Google added 60 languages in one release and took the total to 88 · VnExpress covered it at the time as a straightforward win for foreigners in the country. Download the Vietnamese pack for offline use before you land, because the same camera reads the price list taped inside the window, which is the other document in this transaction.

And occasionally the useful picture is not of nails at all. At Guu Nail Studio, 11 Yên Bái, 59 reviews, a customer turned up with no design in mind. She showed the owner where she was going and what she had packed to wear, along with a few sets she had liked online, and the owner built something different on each nail out of that. It is the most demanding brief anyone in this dataset handed over and it produced one of the warmest reviews in it.

The picture on your phone is not illustrating a preference. It is fixing the price, the hours and the shape in the ninety seconds the two of you spend looking at it, and everything after that is execution. Hold it up before the file comes out, not after.

Frequently asked

Should I bring a photo to a nail salon in Da Nang?

Yes, and send it before you arrive rather than producing it in the chair. 49 of the 715 public reviews behind this guide describe showing a technician a reference picture, across 40 of the 149 salons, and 48 of the 49 are five stars · 31 of them use a word meaning exact correspondence, which is language that barely appears anywhere else in the dataset. A photo carries the colour, the finish, the placement, the length and the shape in one object you can both point at. Most salons here take bookings on WhatsApp, Instagram or Facebook Messenger, so the picture can go in the booking message: one reviewer at a My Khe studio sent hers ahead and arrived to find the design already sampled. Ask to see the shop's own book of designs as well, since everything in it has already been done with the materials on the premises.

Do nail technicians in Da Nang speak English?

Some do and plenty do not, and it matters much less than people expect. Six reviews in this guide's 715 use the phrase language barrier and all six are five stars; seven name Google Translate and all seven are five stars. At a Trần Phú salon with 2,020 Google reviews, a customer's technician spoke no English and two colleagues on the floor translated; at a large salon on Nguyễn Chí Thanh, a customer worked entirely through a translation app and an inspiration photo and reported a better result than she had asked for. Vietnamese has been in Google Translate's instant camera translation since July 2019, so download the offline pack before you land. Learn four words anyway, because they are the ones a picture cannot supply: móng vuông (square), móng tròn (round), móng hạnh nhân (almond), and dài or ngắn for long and short.

How much does nail art cost in Da Nang?

It depends entirely on whether the effect covers the whole set at once or is applied nail by nail, and that is visible in the photo you are holding. Full-set finishes are flat rates on this guide's price tables · cat-eye or chrome around 180K, ombré or French around 220K, on top of a 200K gel colour. Per-nail work multiplies: stickers 10–40K each, hand-painted designs 15–100K, 3D chrome 40–80K, 3D gel flowers 40–70K, pearls and charms 20–80K. Ten hand-painted nails at the top of that band is 1,000K against 180K for a cat-eye set. Real bills reported in the reviews run 300K for extensions with a simple dotted design, 550K for a full set with art, and 850K for extensions with complex work and charms. Count the painted nails in your reference before you agree a number.

How long does a nail appointment with a design take in Da Nang?

Between about an hour and five hours, and the reference photo is what decides it. Reviews in this guide's dataset record a full set of extensions with nail art done in an hour and ten minutes on the My Khe beachfront, an hour and fifteen including removal in Sơn Trà, two and a half hours twice at a studio on Phan Châu Trinh, three hours at an An Thượng salon with two technicians working simultaneously, and five hours at a small room on Phan Châu Trinh where the customer had budgeted four · that last one is the only review in the whole photo set that is not five stars, and the nails themselves were fine. Salons here under-quote time as a habit even without a picture involved: one customer was told thirty minutes for a single-colour gel manicure and sat for two hours. Ask how many hours as well as how much, book complicated work early in the day, and mention any evening plans when you send the photo.
