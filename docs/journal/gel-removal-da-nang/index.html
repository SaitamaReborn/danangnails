Twenty-seven removals in 715 reviews, and the only one-star is about a file

Search the 715 public reviews behind this guide for people who turned up with product already on the nail and 27 come back, spread across 22 of the 149 salons. Twenty-three of the 27 are five stars. One is four, one is three, one is two, and one is one. That distribution is worth more than the count, because removal is the one part of an appointment nobody photographs · there is no before-and-after, no reference picture, nothing to post. When it appears in a review at all, something has usually happened.

The one-star describes a Hải Châu salon where the technician filing off her previous gel was rough enough that she pulled her hand back from the table more than once, because she thought he was about to file into the skin at the base of the nail. Everything else in that review · the polish, the pedicure, the eventual apology from the manager · came after that. The removal was where the visit went wrong, and it happened in the first ten minutes.

The other end of the range is oddly specific in the same way. At a small studio in Sơn Trà, a reviewer writes that the owner takes nails off very gently and is visibly anxious about hurting her. At a shop out on Tôn Đức Thắng in Hòa Khánh, well north-west of anywhere a visitor normally goes, a customer notes that her old manicure came off without her nails being scratched and adds, pointedly, that she had had less successful experiences in Vietnamese salons before. And in An Thượng, someone who had been through an aggressive removal elsewhere came in looking for a softer one, got it, and says in the review that this is why she would trust the same shop to take the next set off as well.

Nobody chooses a salon on its removal. But it is the first thing that happens to you, it involves a spinning bit or a solvent, and it is the only step in the appointment where the shop has nothing to gain from taking its time. That makes it a fairly honest test.

60 to 90K on the menu. 150K on a bill somebody hadn't read.

The price tables put gel removal at 60K to 90K, against roughly 200K for the plain gel colour going on afterwards and 55K for a single tip repair. So at the top of the band removal is not quite half the price of the manicure it precedes, and at the bottom it is less than a third. That is the number to hold.

Reviewers in this dataset confirm it and mostly land under it. One customer at a beachfront salon writes her bill out as two lines · 250K for the manicure itself, 50K for the removal. A nail room on Trần Phú charged 190,000 for a magnetic set with the removal folded in. Another beachfront shop took an old polish off and put fresh gel on in under an hour, walk-in, no appointment, for 240,000 total. At Posh Nail Lab on An Thượng 32, a reviewer had an old set removed and a new manicure done for what she converted to nineteen Australian dollars.

Then the two-star. A customer at a My An salon was quoted 600,000 in a text message before her appointment and billed 750,000 when she got there; the extra 150,000 was removal, which the message had not mentioned. Most of her review is about that gap rather than about her nails. Nothing in it suggests the shop was trying anything on · removal simply was not in the number, because removal frequently is not in the number.

This is exactly why the first of the three questions on the price page is what does removal cost. It is the only line on a nail bill that can be added after you have agreed to a figure, because it is the only work that happens before the work you actually came for. Ask it in the same message where you ask for the appointment, and ask for the total rather than the rate.

Acetone or the drill · and only four reviews in 715 even mention which

Harper's Bazaar Việt Nam ran a piece in February 2024 asking flatly whether gel polish damages nails, and its answer is the useful one: the harm is mechanical, not chemical. To make gel stick, the surface of the nail is filed rough, and that thins it. To get gel off, the two options are an acetone soak, which preserves the nail plate and dries out the skin around it badly, or filing and peeling, which strips keratin along with the product. Almost all of the damage from a gel manicure therefore happens in the ten minutes at the start and the ten at the end, not in the three weeks in between.

Given that, it is striking how invisible the tool is. Exactly four of the 715 reviews here name a drill, an electric file or acetone at all. Customers describe colours, shapes, prices, tea and dogs; they almost never describe what was used to get the last set off.

The four that do are instructive rather than damning, and they point in opposite directions. A visitor to a My Khe beachfront studio explains that her nails are sensitive to acetone, so the technician removed her soft gel using only a sanding bit and was careful with it · the drill used deliberately, for a stated reason, on a client who asked. Against that, a customer at a nail room on Trần Phú arrived with ordinary polish that needed nothing but acetone and a cotton pad, and had an electric file taken to it instead. She gave three stars and was otherwise complimentary about the shop, which is the right verdict: wrong tool, decent salon.

The part that should actually change your behaviour is a two-star at a different Trần Phú address, where a reviewer watched the same nail drill go from her husband's feet to another woman's manicure with no cleaning in between. Files and buffers are cheap, disposable, and get unwrapped in front of you · that is the visible half of the ninety-second check. Drill bits are metal, expensive, reused all day, and cleaned somewhere you cannot see. If a bit is going anywhere near your nail plate, that is the one thing worth asking about, and a shop with a steriliser running will answer it in a sentence.

Say what is on your hands, because the two failures here were both misreads

Not everything comes off the same way, and the difference is not cosmetic. Gel polish and soft gel dissolve in acetone in ten to fifteen minutes under foil. Builder gel and BIAB and GelX tips have to be filed down to a thin translucent layer first and only then soaked, or filed off entirely. Acrylic is filed. A technician who treats a builder set as a soak-off will sit you there for forty minutes with nothing happening; one who treats a plain polish as gel will put a spinning bit on a nail that needed a cotton pad.

Both of the removal complaints in this dataset are that second mistake, and neither is really a competence story. Technicians here read the product off the nail in seconds and are right almost every time. The failure mode is the ambiguous case · an old regular polish that has gone thick and glossy, a soft gel overlay that looks like builder · and the fix costs you one sentence at the start.

The word is worth having. Posted menus in Da Nang write it tháo gel or phá gel, both meaning to take the gel off, and tháo gel bao nhiêu? gets you the price. Note that it appears on most menus as a step rather than as a service with its own heading, which is precisely why it drops out of quotes.

Better still, send it ahead. Messaging beats phoning here by a distance, and 90 of the 149 salons in this guide publish a website or a channel to message. Two photographs settle everything: your hands as they currently are, and the set you want. One reviewer did the whole thing without leaving her hotel · a home-service technician arrived, removed her previous gel, did a gel pedicure and nail art, and was finished in two hours.

Removal has a clock on it, and extensions are the long one

The spread in this dataset is wide enough to plan around. At the fast end, a beachfront walk-in had an old polish removed and a fresh gel colour applied in under an hour for 240,000. At a studio on Trần Phú with two technicians working at once, a customer who specifically asked for a gentle removal because her nails are sensitive had a manicure and a pedicure finished, removal included, in under an hour.

At the slow end, a Hải Châu nail room quoted about 750K for taking off extensions and building a new set, and the appointment ran from half past twelve to half past five. The reviewer gave four stars and said the nails matched her reference photo exactly · she just had not budgeted five hours for it. Another visitor to a Trần Phú studio itemised a fuller job at 750,000 in total: nail design, gel removal, chrome, and gel plus removal on the toes as well.

What varies is not the shop, it is what is coming off. Colour comes off fast. Structure does not. If you arrived with extensions and want extensions, treat the whole thing as a half-day and pick a salon you would be happy sitting in.

Which argues for the morning, as most things here do. 118 of the 145 salons publishing hours are working by half past nine, and a nine o'clock removal on the first full day of a trip costs you nothing except the morning. The same job squeezed in at four in the afternoon before a flight is the version that goes wrong.

The real risk in Da Nang is not a bad salon. It is the arithmetic.

Start with how long a set actually lasts here, because the reviews are unusually forthcoming about it. Reports in this dataset run two weeks, three weeks, three and a half, three to four, and two separate accounts of six · one from a beachfront salon where the customer came back specifically because the last set had held that long. A visitor to An Thượng had an eight-week set taken off. At the other end, a one-star at a Hải Châu shop describes a set peeling within a week with dirt working in underneath. Three to four weeks is the honest planning figure.

Now put that next to the price. A gel colour is 200K. That is roughly the cost of two decent coffees on Bạch Đằng, and it is low enough that the natural rhythm of a long stay becomes a new set whenever you fancy one rather than a new set when the old one has grown out. Harper's Bazaar Việt Nam suggests a minimum two-week rest between applications. Long Châu, the pharmacy chain with a shopfront on half the corners in Hải Châu, goes considerably further and puts the sensible ceiling at five or six polishes a year · a figure nobody on a beach holiday is going to observe, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.

Here is the flag, though. The thing most likely to leave you with thin, sore nails after a month in this city is not a careless salon. It is three sets in four weeks because each one cost eight dollars, and each of those three began with the nail plate being filed rough and the last set being taken off. High skill and low prices are a genuinely good combination, and they are also exactly the conditions under which people over-service their own hands.

One last thing, and it is the cheapest good decision available here. Do not fly home with three weeks of lifted gel to pick at. Removal is 60K to 90K · under four dollars · and takes twenty minutes on its own. Book it for the morning of your flight or the day before, walk out with bare nails, buffed and oiled, and let them have the fortnight off.

Frequently asked

How much does gel removal cost in Da Nang?

60K to 90K on posted menus, against about 200K for the gel colour that usually follows it. Reviewers in this guide's dataset record it lower in practice · one itemises 50K for removal beside a 250K manicure, another had an old set removed and a fresh manicure done for the equivalent of nineteen Australian dollars. The thing to watch is not the rate but whether it is in your quote: one customer was messaged 600,000 before her appointment and billed 750,000, and the 150,000 difference was removal that nobody had mentioned. Ask for the total, not the rate, and ask in writing.

Can I get gel nails done in another country removed in Da Nang?

Yes, and it is routine · 27 of the 715 reviews behind this guide describe exactly that, across 22 different salons, and 23 of them are five stars. The one sentence worth preparing is what is actually on your nails. Gel polish and soft gel soak off in acetone in ten to fifteen minutes; builder gel, BIAB, GelX and acrylic have to be filed down to a thin layer first. Both removal complaints in this dataset came from a product being read wrong at the start, not from an unskilled technician.

Do Da Nang nail salons use a drill or acetone to remove gel?

Both, and only four of 715 reviews here mention which · the tool is largely invisible to customers. Harper's Bazaar Việt Nam's February 2024 piece on gel damage is clear that the harm from gel is mechanical rather than chemical: filing the nail rough at the start, and filing or peeling the product off at the end. An acetone soak protects the nail plate and dries the surrounding skin badly. A drill in trained hands is fine · one reviewer with an acetone sensitivity had her soft gel taken off with a sanding bit and praised the care. The question worth asking is not drill or acetone, it is how the drill bits are cleaned, because unlike files and buffers they are metal, reused, and cleaned out of sight.

Should I get my nails removed before flying home from Da Nang?

If the set is more than three weeks old, yes. Removal here is 60K to 90K, which is under four dollars and less than a bottle of remover costs at home, and it takes about twenty minutes as a standalone appointment. The alternative is picking lifted gel off on the plane, which is the single worst thing you can do to a nail plate · it takes keratin with it. Book it for the morning: 118 of the 145 salons publishing hours in this guide are open by half past nine, so a removal slot on your last morning is easy to get and hard to get wrong.
